The model is the engine

ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are apps: the car. Inside each sits a model, the engine that writes the answer, and an app can swap its engine.

It reads in tokens

Text is cut into small pieces called tokens, and you pay for every token in and every token out.

Some engines are open

Open weights models can be downloaded and run on your own servers. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM 5.3 are two.

Problem 1 · India

A speech model, a local language model and a small fast one.

The call
  1. A speech model hears the request
  2. A model trained on Indian languages reads it
  3. A small fast model picks the action
  4. The UPI PIN approves the payment, not a model
Rule out
A frontier model for every request: too slow for an answer within a second, and too costly at millions a day.
Test it on
Recordings from real users in each language and accent; code checks the payee and the amount.
Switch when
A language misses your bar, or a cheaper model passes the same recordings.

PM angleKeep the riskiest step out of the model: it proposes, the PIN decides.

Problem 2 · United States

A small model for most chats, a large one for the hard ones.

The call
  1. Embeddings find the policy for the booking
  2. A small fast model answers most chats
  3. A frontier model takes the hard ones
  4. A person takes what the models hand up
Rule out
A frontier model on every chat: most chats are routine, and the bill grows with each one.
Test it on
Last month’s chats, each graded on whether the answer followed the policy.
Switch when
The cost per resolved chat climbs, or a cheaper model passes the same chats.

PM anglePrice the resolved chat, not the token: a cheap model that hands half its chats to people is not cheap.

Problem 3 · Italy

A frontier model where the data may go, or an open one at home.

The call
  1. Embeddings find the pages that matter
  2. A frontier model in an EU region writes the summary, or open weights do it on the bank’s own servers
  3. Code links every number to the page it came from
Rule out
A small model to save money: one wrong number in a credit decision costs more than the model does.
Test it on
Files with known answers; code checks each number against its source page.
Switch when
The rules on where data may go change, or an open model passes at a lower cost.

PM angleWhere the data may go narrows the shortlist before any score does.

Problem 4 · Anywhere

Not a language model at all.

The call
  1. A prediction model trained on your own trips gives the minutes

Uber’s DeepETA works this way: it corrects its route planner’s estimate, and must answer “within a few milliseconds at most”.

Rule out
The route planner alone: it knows the roads, not last month’s real trips.
Test it on
Last month’s orders: minutes off on average, and how often you were late.
Switch when
The errors grow in a new city, a new season or a new kind of order.

PM angleWhen the answer is a number from your own data, a language model is the slow and costly way to get it.

Level 6 of 6The evaluation

How do you score a model, and when do you switch?

Fig. 7 · Judging a model, in five layers
Layer 1 · The answer you see

The answer you see: one good reply proves little.

Scored byNothing. One reply is one sample, and the next reply can differ.

Behind every reply sits a model trained twice: first to continue text, then tuned on questions, answers and people’s rankings of them, with safety checks before and after. One impressive answer tests none of that.

Source · ByteByteGo

PM angleDemos sell; distributions ship. One reply is an anecdote, not a measurement.

Layer 2 · The leaderboard

The leaderboard: a composite, and it moves.

Scored byTests with checked answers, each scored as the share it gets right.

Artificial Analysis rolls nine evaluations into one index, a quarter each for knowledge work, coding, science and a general set. Between 23 and 29 October, as the index moved to version 5, Kimi K3.1 Pro went from 55 and fourth place to 46.

Sources · Artificial Analysis · OfficeChai

PM angleRead which tasks make the score. A model built for your job can rank lower on someone else’s mix.

The arena: what people prefer, side by side.

Scored byAnonymous votes, turned into a rating, as in chess.

Layer 4 · Your own evals

Your own evals: the only test that matches your users.

Scored byThe share of your own cases that pass.

Real cases from your product, graded by code, a person or a judge model, and rerun before every change and on live traffic. In a 2023 study, strong judge models agreed with people over 80% of the time, as often as people agreed with each other.

The true pass rate is likely between 85% and 93%.

Sources · PracHub · arXiv

PM angleThe eval set is a product asset. It outlives every model it is used to choose.

Layer 5 · Cost, speed, control

Cost, speed and control: the bill is a feature.

Scored byDollars per task, seconds to the first word, and where the data may go.

Price per token is not price per task: Claude Sonnet 5.5 costs half as much as Opus 5.5 per token and the same per task. Effort is a dial too: Opus 5.5 scores 48 at medium for $2.45 a task, and 58 at max for $10.04.

Source · Artificial Analysis

The PM lens

“Explain what evals are for an AI product built on large language models, and how they work in practice.”

Intuit PM interview · machine learning · reported to PracHub, 30 August 2026
What the round tests

Whether you can turn “is it good?” into a test a team runs every week.

  1. Start from one feature and what good looks like.Write down the cases that matter, the awkward ones included, each with what a good answer must contain.
  2. Decide who grades each answer.Code checks for formats and facts that can be matched; people for judgement; a model acting as judge for scale, checked against people.
  3. Use the evals twice.As a gate before any new model or prompt ships, and on a weekly sample of live answers, so drift shows before complaints do.
If this goes wrong

Trust the leaderboard instead, and the model that tops it on science papers can fail your customers’ questions. Nobody notices until the complaints arrive.

Watch
Pass rate on your cases · cost per resolved request · answers people correct or reject
With
Engineering · data science · the domain experts who write the cases · legal for risky answers
The decision inside

Pick the cheapest model that passes your own evals.

  1. StopThree leaders within two points of each other.And a different winner on the human vote.
  2. NoticeThe same price per token can mean a very different price per task.And a new version of a test can move a model nine points in a week.
  3. TradeoffOne top model for everything, or the right model for each job.Simplicity, or a smaller bill.
  4. DecideRoute each job to the cheapest model that passes your cases, and run the cases again whenever a model or prompt changes.What it cost: a test set to build and keep current, and prompts that must work on more than one model.
