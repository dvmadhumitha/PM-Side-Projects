A PM’s city plan

How would a product manager plan a city today?

A design thinking exercise with a mentor: an empty site, ten lakh people, and every part of a city to place. I approached it the way I would scope a product: the market first, then the clusters, then the system.

Stopped at
A blank site, one number, and no way to tell a good plan from a bad one.
Noticed
People use a city like an app: a few things daily, the rest rarely.
Tradeoff
Every layout is worse at something. Which edge case can you live with?
Decided
Two lines, fifty five residential clusters, and one city centre where they cross.

The PM lens

“Design a subway system. Treat it as a product design problem: decide what the system is for and whom it must serve.”

Coinbase PM interview · product design and strategy · reported to PracHub, updated 23 October 2026
What the round tests

Whether you start from the trip a person makes, door to door, rather than from the trains.

  1. Name the riders before the line.A satellite of ten lakh beside Hyderabad, where most of the morning peak leaves for the city. Decide who the system is for first: the commuter, the child going to school, the person who cannot drive.
  2. Walk the whole trip, door to door.Most trips break before the platform. So every home sits within 800 m of a stop, and no daily errand crosses an arterial road.
  3. With one budget, buy frequency first.The round’s follow up offers an extension, more trains or step free stations. More trains on the busiest line helps every rider on day one; step free access comes next, and the extension waits for the district it serves.
If this goes wrong

A year in, ridership is far below the forecast. Look before the platform: the walk, the crossing, the fare. A stop nobody can reach on foot is a stop nobody uses.

Decided

Ask six questions first, so a layout can fail. Design one cluster well. Let the lines place the rest.
