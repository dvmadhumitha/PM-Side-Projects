Boarding Pass Design Analysis

Who is a boarding pass actually written for?

One printed document, several readers, and part of its information changes after it is printed. A teardown of what the pass carries, who reads it, and when. Eight steps; the drawing redraws itself as you go.

Stopped at
One pass, three readers, the same ink for every field.
Noticed
Gate and boarding time change after printing, and look like fields that never do.
Tradeoff
One artefact for every reader, or a layout per reader.
Decided
Split by static, dynamic and becomes dynamic. Then by who is reading.
01 · The pass as printed

Nineteen fields, one weight

A domestic single segment flight, BLR to DEL on IndiGo 6E, departing 08:15. The pass is issued at check in and carried to the exit.

Everything on it is printed at the same size. The PNR, the gate and a partner offer share one typeface and one weight, so nothing leads the eye.

DecidedStart from the information, not the layout.

02 · The readers

Ten people touch the trip. Three read the pass to decide something.

One rule did the shortlisting: keep the readers who make a decision about the passenger from the pass itself, at a defined moment. Operations and handlers work from systems and bag tags. Terminal entry checks ticket and ID. Cabin crew read it, but nothing depends on the reading.

DecidedPassenger, security officer, gate agent. Only name and flight are read by all three.

03 · The journey

Six moments where the pass comes out

Check in issues it. Security reads it once, in a moving queue. Between security and the gate the passenger reads it repeatedly, for wayfinding. The gate agent scans and glances. After landing it is still in hand: domestic arrivals in India check the baggage tag against the bag at the exit.

Until about 2010 the pass changed mid journey. The gate agent tore it, and the stub carried only what the next phase needed.

DecidedTreat the phase after boarding as a phase.

04 · Who reads what

Each reader uses a different subset

Security reads name, flight and date, against a photo ID. The gate agent reads group and sequence, then the barcode. The passenger reads gate, boarding time and seat, over and over, between security and the aircraft door.

The fields the passenger reads most are printed no larger than the ones nobody reads. The partner offer is the only field none of the three uses.

DecidedSize a field by the next decision its reader makes from it.

05 · Known at issuance?

One question sorts every field

A · Static. Known when issued and unchanged: name, flight, date, route, PNR, class, group, sequence, baggage, terminal, barcode. B · Dynamic. Not known when issued: gate, arrival terminal, belt. C · Static, then changes. Printed as fact, can move: departure time, boarding time, seat.

Bucket C is why the split is three way. Those fields print like A and behave like B. A two way sort hides them.

DecidedThree buckets, not two.

06 · What moves after printing

The two most read fields are the two most likely to change

A gate change or a delay rewrites gate and boarding time. On today's pass they carry the same ink as the PNR, which never changes. Meanwhile the baggage tag reference, fixed at check in and the one thing checked at the exit, arrives as a sticker on the back.

DecidedPrint what is fixed. Put what can change behind a live layer.

07 · Redrawn

Split by reader, weighted by the next decision

An identity strip on top for security: name, flight, date, one glance. The passenger's block large: route, gate, boarding time, seat, and gate close as a clock time rather than small print. Group, sequence and the code on the right for the gate agent. The baggage tag reference printed in, not stuck on.

DecidedSame artefact, three zones, one reading order per reader.

08 · On a phone

The same split, live by default

On a mobile pass the live layer is the screen. Gate and boarding time update in place and say so. The identity strip and the gate agent's code keep their places, so the reading order survives the change of medium. Paper remains the fallback, and what is essential has to survive on paper alone.

DecidedDesign for the reading conditions: a moving queue, one hand busy, no time.

What it changes in practice

The method is not about boarding passes. Any artefact several people read under time pressure sorts the same way: who reads it, when, to decide what, and which of its fields can change after it was made.

A payment receipt, a prescription, a delivery label and an order confirmation all have a bucket C. They print fixed, then change, and the reader finds out late.
