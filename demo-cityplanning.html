Zone it yourself

Five zone types, one grid, one rail line down the middle. Pick a zone and drag across the board — the panel keeps score on three things residents actually notice. It opens on a deliberately bad city: the works sit at the western edge, and the wind blows west to east, so everyone downwind is breathing it. See if you can fix it.

Row 3 is the rail spine — the dark squares are stations, and you can't build on the line. Wind runs west → east, so anything east of a factory is downwind of it.

How it lives

WHAT IT'S SHOWING

The same grid, read as a screen

Nothing below is redrawn — it's your grid, rotated into a layout. Bands near the rail line become the parts of a screen a thumb reaches. Whatever you buried in the corners is what someone will have to go looking for.

Offices → primary action. Whatever you stacked densest at the interchange is what the screen leads with.

Industry → destructive action. If it ended up next to housing, it's next to the thing people tap every day.

Parks and civic → escape hatches. Distributed, or one big one nobody's near?

The rail band → the thumb zone. Everything you put on or beside it is what a user can reach without moving their hand.

What building this taught me

Almost everyone's first city sorts by type — all the housing here, all the industry there. It scores badly on every axis, and it's the exact layout I'd have shipped as a settings page.

Grouping by what a thing is feels rigorous. Grouping by what someone is trying to do is the one that survives contact. That's the whole argument of the case study.
