Sized by value

You should be able to tell them apart without looking

The rupee already grows in length, by 27 mm from ₹10 to ₹500. Its height barely moves, so two notes in a wallet feel the same between finger and thumb. Grow both edges, and the difference is in the hand before it is in the eye.

Reference points

Teardowns of 5 currencies: Euro, USD, UAE Dirham, Swiss Franc, Norwegian Krone
ISO / IMF / IBNS standards, what a note must carry vs what's tradition
Paper money since 618 AD China, which elements survived a millennium, and why

What each currency taught us

Euro 🇪🇺every denomination a different size, and it grows with value. Tactile ridges on the high notes. No real people on it: bridges and arches that belong to no single member state.
US dollar 🇺🇸every note the same size, near enough the same colour. A US court found in 2008 this failed blind and low vision users. The counter example, not the model.
Swiss franc 🇨🇭 · vertical. Same width, different height per denomination. Themes rather than portraits. The one that made us take the rotation seriously.
UAE dirham 🇦🇪fully bilingual, Arabic and English, numerals in both systems on both faces. Closest parallel to India's two script mandate.
Norwegian krone 🇳🇴abstract pixel mosaic reverse instead of a face. Proof a note can carry identity without a portrait.

What we observed

Everything on today's note shouts at once, no information hierarchy
Accessibility is bolted on: tactile marks wear off, colours fail colour blind users
One tiny canvas vs bilingual mandate + regional scripts + security features
Real life eats notes: wallet folds, ATM rollers, counting machines, monsoons

Converging

Every element sorted: must have / good to have / use case based
Two structural approaches drafted; vertical orientation flagged as the emerging trend
Colour per denomination (₹20 amber, ₹100 green), checked against colour blindness
7 typefaces tested: display faces for numerals, workhorses for scripts

Must have Good to have Use case based
01 · The sort

Every element, ranked before it was drawn.

Security, accessibility, two scripts, machine readability and cultural neutrality all compete for a few square centimetres. The way through was not a better drawing. It was an inventory: every element on the note, force ranked, split by front and back.

RankElementWhy it is there
Must haveThe numeralFirst, high contrast, checked against colour blind palettes. Value reads before words do.
Must haveThe value in both scriptsPer the constitutional mandate, in a fixed order so neither crowds the other.
Must haveThe tactile markOne pattern per value, at the held edge, clear of the fold line and the ATM roller.
Must haveThe security threadNot negotiable. Placed where folds will not wear it.
Use caseThe serialFor machines. The same position on every denomination, because counting machines and ATMs are users too, and they have no patience.
Good to havePortrait and motifMoved off the face. Nothing stays on the front that is only good to have.

Four must haves, one use case element. Seven typefaces were tested to get the numerals and the two scripts to sit together: display faces for the numerals, workhorses for the scripts.

02 · Take this with you

The same sort works on a screen.

  • Design for the least served user first. Here that was the visually impaired. It raised the floor for everyone.
  • List the machines that read your artefact too. Parsers, scanners and printers are users with no patience.
  • Stress test against real life: wallet folds, rollers, monsoons, and the designed for edge case, demonetisation.

List everything on it. Rank it. Cut or layer everything below the line. Then benchmark four or five comparable products element by element, not vibe by vibe, and you find out what is convention and what is necessary.

Decided

Sized by value, vertical, thumb first. The draft that survived the wallet, the roller and the monsoon.
