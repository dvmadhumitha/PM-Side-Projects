Rethinking the currency note

We use it every day. Have we ever asked why it looks the way it does?

I compared ten notes from around the world, took today’s ₹500 apart, then sorted, placed and sized every part again: for people who see a note, see it poorly, or only feel it.

Stopped at
₹10, ₹20 and ₹50 carry no mark a finger can find.
Noticed
From ₹10 to ₹500 the note grows 27 mm longer, but only 3 mm taller.
Tradeoff
Every mark for touch or sight takes room from security and the two scripts.
Decided
One shape at six sizes, a raised code, and the value in every quarter.

What is on today’s ₹500, and why there?

The note as the RBI describes it: thirteen parts on the front, five on the back.

The product as it ships
Fig. 2 · Today’s ₹500, and the job each part does
  • Value3 · 10 · 18

    Read at 3 and 10; held to the light at 1, 2 and 8; felt at 12. On the back, 18.

  • Trust4 · 7 · 11

    A face, a signed promise and the state’s seal, side by side.

  • Security1 · 2 · 5 · 6 · 8

    Each needs blank paper or light behind it: the left end and the watermark field.

  • Touch12 · 13

    A raised circle on the right, five raised bleed lines on each side.

  • Machines9

    Serials in opposite corners, the digits growing, hard to alter.

  • Story14 to 17

    The back: a monument, fifteen languages, a campaign, the year.

The PM lens

“Design an ATM that people who are blind can use on their own.”

Google PM interview · product design and strategy · reported to PracHub, updated 28 October 2026
What the round tests

Whether you follow the person from start to finish, rather than designing the machine.

  1. Start with the cash, not the machine.A talking ATM is no use if the note it pays out can’t be checked by hand. The lower rupee notes can’t be.
  2. Give every value three cues that work alone.Size, a raised code and colour. The round’s first follow up asks about people who don’t read Braille: the code is dots to count, not Braille, as on Canada’s notes.
  3. Ship into the machines already out there.The second follow up: banks won’t replace their machines quickly. So the ₹500 stays 150 mm long, as now, and the new values arrive one at a time.
If this goes wrong

A year in, blind users still check every note with an app. Test whether the dots survive a year in a wallet: a mark that wears flat is a mark nobody trusts.

Watch
Notes named right by touch alone, in a blind test · counting errors at bank counters · mixed up notes reported
With
The RBI’s currency department · the note presses · associations of blind people · ATM makers · banks
Decided

Make the value impossible to miss: by eye, by hand, or folded in a wallet.

  1. StopAlmost every part of today’s note needs sight.Touch arrived late, and only on the higher values.
  2. NoticeThe best notes code value by count or place, not by Braille.The pound counts dots; the yen moves one mark.
  3. TradeoffRoom for touch and sight comes out of security and two scripts.So the portrait moves into the watermark.
  4. DecideOne shape at six sizes, a three dot code, one numeral in every quarter.What it cost: the face at the centre, and new sizes for every machine.
