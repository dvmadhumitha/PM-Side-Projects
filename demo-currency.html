What this demonstrates

Designing under hard constraints. Security, accessibility, bilingual mandate, machine-readability and cultural neutrality all compete for the same few square centimetres. The full exploration benchmarks five currencies against ISO/IMF/IBNS standards, tests seven typefaces, and iterates ₹10/₹20/₹100 drafts across two structural approaches — including vertical orientation, the emerging global trend.

Behind the scenesWhat I actually did

  1. Benchmarked before designing: tore down the Euro, USD, Swiss Franc, Dirham and Krone front-and-back — what every element does and for whom — against ISO/IMF/IBNS standards.
  2. Built the information inventory: every element on a note force-ranked into must-have / good-to-have / use-case-based, split by front and back.
  3. Iterated ₹10/₹20/₹100 drafts across two structural approaches — horizontal and vertical — testing seven typefaces and per-denomination colour coding.
  4. Stress-tested against real life: colour-blind palettes, tactile-mark wear, wallet folds, ATM rollers, counting machines — and kept demonetisation as the designed-for edge case.

Take this with youIf your artifact is overcrowded

  • Inventory every element on the screen/document, then force-rank: must-have / good-to-have / use-case. Cut or layer everything below the line.
  • Benchmark 4–5 comparable products element by element, not vibe by vibe — you'll find what's convention vs what's necessary.
  • Design for the least-served user first (here: visually-impaired). Accessibility decisions raise the floor for everyone.
  • List the machines that read your artifact too — parsers, scanners, printers are users with zero patience.
