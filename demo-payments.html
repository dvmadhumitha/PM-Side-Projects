01 · Rail selection

Follow one payment

Pick a rail and a market. The $100 stops at everyone who takes a cut, and you see what reaches the shop.

Through a PM’s lens
“Walk me through the bookkeeping that sits behind a payments processor.”
Stripe · technical round
Three moves
  1. Two rows, never oneEvery movement is a debit and a matching credit.
  2. Authorisation is not settlementOn a card the ledger holds money that is promised and not yet moved, for T+1 to T+3.
  3. Derive balances, never store themAppend events and compute the total.

If this goes wrongThe follow up is always “what happens when the payment reverses”. A ledger that cannot express a reversal gets rebuilt.

      Three things that aren’t a rail

      Cash. No instruction, no ledger, no fee.

      Closed loop balances. M Pesa and the like. A book entry on one company’s ledger that never touches a bank rail.

      Stablecoins. Settle on a public ledger instead of a bank one. PayPal and Stripe are both betting on them for cross border.

      03 · Market expansion

      Where each one wins

      Cards won where they arrived first. Bank transfers won where someone built a rail and made it free. Wallets won where the phone came before the bank.

      Through a PM’s lens
      “Brazil checkout converts at half of Italy. Same funnel, same copy.”
      Growth, after a launch
      Three moves
      1. Read the method mix, not the funnelPix carries 42% of online value in Brazil.
      2. Rank by coverage, not by effortOne method at 40% share beats three at 5%.
      3. Sequence the buildOne method shipped well beats four half integrated.

      If this goes wrongShip the wrong method first and you rebuild a checkout that was never going to convert there.

      WatchPayment method mix · Checkout completion by market

      Worldpay 2026 · NPCI Aug 2026 · McKinsey 2025 · World Bank Q3 2025. Country outlines follow lines of de facto control, not any government’s official map.

      Cards. You borrow the shop’s trust and settle later. Four parties, a percentage fee, and the only rail here a private company owns.

      Account to account. Money moves between two bank accounts over a bank rail, settled at the central bank. UPI, Pix, iDEAL, Swish, BLIK. A licensed bank at each end; the balance is a bank deposit.

      Wallets. Usually not a rail at all. A front end over a card or a bank transfer. Apple Pay and Google Pay are tokenised cards. Alipay and WeChat Pay are the exception: in China they really are the ledger.

      Mobile money. The balance lives on a telco’s ledger instead. M Pesa, MTN MoMo, Airtel Money. Cash in at an agent, send to a phone number, cash out at another agent. One pooled trust account at a real bank sits behind the whole float.

      Cash. No instruction, no ledger, no fee, no data. Still the default in a tenth of the countries on this map.

      Why the last two matter most. Mobile money works with no bank account and no smartphone, USSD on a feature phone is enough, and it charges the sender on a tiered scale. Account to account is usually free but assumes everyone already banks. In both, the distribution network is the moat, not the technology.

      Three markets I am acquainted with

      Three markets I have lived in or built for. Two of them run on identical card rails. The cost differs only because the regulation does.

      04 · Business model & licensing

      Who bills whom, and for how much

      Each company prices a different part of the payment. Where the payment itself is free, the money moves one step sideways.

      Through a PM’s lens
      “Design an API that splits one payment across several sellers.”
      Stripe · senior PM round
      Three moves
      1. Decide if you touch the moneyHolding it means a licence and a percentage. Not holding it means a flat fee.
      2. Model both at your real volumeA flat fee overtakes a percentage somewhere near $1m a month.
      3. Find the second line earlyWhat you sell beside the payment is the only part that survives a fee cap.

      If this goes wrongSatispay charged a flat 20 cents for years, then had to reprice its entire merchant base in April 2025.

      If UPI is almost free, who gets paid?

      The money did not disappear. It moved one step sideways, and that move is the Indian payments business.

      Italy ran the same experiment, and blinked

      Satispay built a bank transfer wallet on the opposite pricing idea: free below €10, a flat twenty cents above it. It worked well enough to reach a few million users, then the arithmetic caught up.

      05 · Compliance & risk

      The rules on top, and what they make you build

      Both big markets have arrived at the same place: instant money, a name check before you send, consented data sharing, near zero fees. Europe legislated toward it. India built it first and wrote the rules afterwards. The destination converges; the route decides what you can ship, and how long you have.

      Through a PM’s lens
      “Verification of payee has been law since October. Our name match is rejecting valid payees.”
      Compliance, with a date
      Three moves
      1. Turn the rule into a screenThe law says show the real name. The near match threshold is yours.
      2. Price the exemptionBasis points of fraud prevented against points of checkout abandoned.
      3. Work backwards from the dateIndia’s UPI pricing changes on 15 Oct 2026. It will not move for anyone’s sprint.

      If this goes wrongMiss a payments deadline and you are not late, you are unlicensed.

      One checkout, and who has already decided each step for you

      The same five moments in every card checkout on earth. In three markets, three different people have made the call before your team ever opens Figma.

      What is already true, and what lands next

      The useful way to read a payments regulator is as a dated dependency. Here it is, in the order it hits a roadmap.

      Already in force
      • Europe · since Oct 2025Instant euro transfers may not cost more than slow ones, and the bank must show you the payee’s real name before you confirm.You design the near match screen and decide what “close enough” looks like.
      • India · since Apr 2026Two factors on every domestic digital payment, one of them dynamic, but the RBI now allows risk based alternatives to the SMS OTP.Somebody has to pick which alternative, and own the fraud number.
      • United States · no equivalentInstant is optional. Only debit interchange is capped. There is no two factor mandate.The market decides, which means your competitors do.
      Still coming
      • 15 Oct 2026 · IndiaUPI stops being free for everyone. A 0.4% fee, capped ₹300, applies to payments over ₹2,000 to large merchants. Small merchants and anything under ₹2,000 stay at zero.Six years of “you cannot charge for this” just became “you can, a bit, to some people”. Reprice accordingly.
      • 13 Nov 2026 · IndiaConsent Manager registration opens under the data protection rules.The first piece of the data law that needs a build, not a policy.
      • 31 Dec 2026 · IndiaNo single UPI app may carry more than 30% of volume. From 2027 an app above the line has to stop signing up new users.PhonePe and Google Pay are both far above it. Deferred twice already.
      • 13 May 2027 · IndiaIndia’s data protection law in full: notice, consent, deletion, breach reporting, retention limits.Every screen that collects anything gets revisited.
      • ~2028 · EuropePSD3 and the Payment Services Regulation. Texts were agreed in April 2026 but are not adopted yet; most rules apply about 21 months after publication.Long enough to plan for, close enough to stop pretending it is not coming.
      • No date · United StatesThe open banking rule is enjoined and being rewritten. The new draft may let banks charge for access to customer data.If your product reads bank data, your cost base is an open question.

      Decided

      Three markets, three answers, none of them about technology.

      1. StopPulled, pushed, batched, settled one at a time, relayed abroad.
      2. NoticeCards where they arrived first; free public rails where someone built one; wallets where the phone came before the bank.
      3. TradeoffEvery rail is a symptom of this one constraint.
