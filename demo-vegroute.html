How to read this

A routing problem, not a search problem.

Every map app answers "vegetarian restaurants near me". The question I have when travelling is different: I am going from here to there, where can I eat without leaving the road? A radius around a point cannot answer that. A corridor along a line can.

The route comes back from OSRM as a few thousand points. Every candidate place is measured to its nearest point on the line, and anything beyond the corridor is dropped. A radius around the midpoint would surface places twenty minutes off route.

Diet labels come from the diet:vegetarian and diet:vegan tags, which are contributed rather than advertised. Outside the cities they are sparse. The honest version shows what the data says and how far off the road it is, rather than inventing a confidence score.

How it works, and what I chose

No API keys and no paid services. The whole thing runs on open data, which was a deliberate constraint rather than a limitation I worked around.

A corridor, not a radius

The route geometry comes back from OSRM as a few thousand points. Every candidate place is measured to its nearest point on the line, and anything beyond 500 m is dropped. A radius around the midpoint would surface places you'd have to drive twenty minutes off route to reach.

Ask the map, not a brand

Places come from OpenStreetMap via Overpass: restaurants, cafés, fast food, bars, ice cream and bakeries in the route's bounding box. Diet labels come from the diet:vegetarian and diet:vegan tags, which are contributed rather than advertised.

The label is the hard part

In Italy a dish marked vegetariano can still arrive with something I don't eat. OSM's tags are sparse and inconsistent, so the honest version shows what the data actually claims and how far off route it is, rather than inventing a confidence score the data can't support.

The extension does the same thing inside openstreetmap.org: draw a route there and a sidebar appears with the stops along it. This page is that logic lifted out and pointed at the same public APIs, so you can try it without installing anything.

The label is the hard part

Show what the data claims, not a score it cannot support

In Italy a dish marked vegetariano can still arrive with something I don't eat. The tags are sparse and inconsistent, especially outside the cities. So the panel shows three things per stop and nothing else: what the data says about it, how far it is from the road, and nothing that looks like a verdict.

Stops are drawn where the data puts them. Where the data is thin the list is short, and the page says so. That is the cost of the choice, and it is a cost I would pay again: a short honest list beats a long list with a made up score.

Decided

A corridor, not a radius. Open data, and show the gaps instead of hiding them.
