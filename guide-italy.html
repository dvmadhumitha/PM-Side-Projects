  1. People58.9m

    residents on 1 January 2026. Arrivals from abroad made up for 296,000 more deaths than births.

  2. Age49.1

    median age, against 44.9 across the EU. One resident in four is 65 or older.

  3. Births355,000

    babies born in 2025: 1.14 children per woman, a record low.

  4. Newcomers9.4%

    of residents are foreign citizens: 11.7% in the north, 5.0% in the south.

  5. Towns7,894

    comuni. Seven in ten have fewer than 5,000 people; together they hold one resident in six.

    regions. Five have special autonomy under the constitution, with their own statutes.

03 · Through a PM’s lens

Through a PM’s lens

Italy is the harder version of the question: price and supply, the two levers ride hailing normally pulls, are both written into law.

Through a PM’s lens
“What would you do in a market like Germany with heavy regulatory compliance?”Uber · Senior PM · hiring manager screen · reported on Exponent, 2026

Uber is the world’s largest ride hailing app. In Italy its cheap service with ordinary drivers was banned in 2015; today the app sells licensed hire cars and, since 2022, other people’s taxis.

Italy is the harder version of the question. Each city caps its taxi licences and sets the fares, and the only other legal option, a hire car with a driver, must go back to its garage between rides, under a 1992 law.

So the two levers ride hailing normally pulls, price and supply, are both written into law.

The same problem atUberFreeNowitTaxiWetaxi

Three moves
  1. Map who writes the rules before you map the ridersHere that is four layers: Parliament, the transport ministry, the region and the comune. A ministry decree made hire cars wait 20 minutes between booking and pickup; in November 2025 the Constitutional Court struck the rule down.
  2. Rent the supply you are not allowed to buildUber stopped fighting the taxis and put them in its app. The itTaxi deal of 2022, renewed in February 2026 until 2030, covers more than 10,000 taxis in about 50 comuni and has carried over 20 million requests. Uber’s cut: about 6%.
  3. Compete where the city does not set the priceThe comune fixes the fare, so price is not a lever. Pickup time, certainty and payment are, worth most at airports and big events, which is the very take home Uber set the same candidate.
If this goes wrong

A court switches the product off. A Milan court ruled UberPop unfair competition on 26 May 2015 and ordered it blocked; on 9 July the appeal confirmed the ban across Italy. The riders were there; the licence was not.

Two systems, side by side

An Italian phone runs two systems side by side: a public one that knows exactly who you are, and a private one that mostly comes from abroad.
