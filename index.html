Legend

Four questions. Every time.

Banknote, lift panel or payment rail, same four questions. The third is the one I am most likely to skip, so I write it down.

Q1

What made me stop?

Written as a question, so you can check it rather than take my word for it.

Q2

What did I notice?

Usually the point where the obvious answer stops working.

Q3

What can't be had both ways?

The tradeoff. The decision is which edge case you can live with.

Q4

What did I decide?

A position, and what it cost.

A bit about me

Senior Product Manager at a fintech startup, with corporate years before that. I've built for users in India and for global markets, and the job is the same everywhere: find the real problem before building for it.

Most things are the way they are for a reason. I like finding out what that reason was, and whether it still holds. This page is the after hours version of that.

Some of it started with problems of my own. Preparing for my Italian exam, I found plenty to study but nowhere to sit a timed practice run, so I built one. New to a city and vegetarian, I wanted places to eat along my route from one point to another, or a short detour off it, not just whatever was nearby.

The rest is curiosity about everyday objects most of us stopped noticing. Redrawing one is the most honest way I know to find out what the original was solving for.
