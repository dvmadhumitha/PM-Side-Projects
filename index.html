Language Exam Simulator

Timed practice for Italian certification exams — built around exam-day conditions rather than vocabulary drills.

What I did: Started it while preparing for my own certification. Designed the practice flow around the clock, the section order and the pressure, since that is what I kept failing to rehearse.

Problem — Plenty of apps teach the language. Far fewer rehearse the exam: fixed timing, fixed section order, no going back.

Approach — Worked backwards from the exam format instead of from the syllabus, and treated the countdown as the core mechanic rather than a feature.

What it shows — Designing for a constraint someone else set, and building the thing I actually needed rather than the thing that was easier to build.

Veg Route

Enter where you're starting and where you're heading, and see the vegetarian and vegan places along the way — with the local labelling translated into what you can actually eat.

What I did: Began it after moving to Italy, where a dish marked vegetarian can still arrive with something I don't eat. Mapping the gap between how food is labelled and what a newcomer can rely on.

Problem — Existing filters answer "is there a vegetarian restaurant nearby". The question I actually have is "along this route, where can I eat, and can I trust the label".

Approach — Treating it as a routing problem rather than a search problem, and building a confidence signal for labelling instead of a simple yes/no tag.

What it shows — Starting from a real, repeated problem, and resisting the version that is easier to build than to use.

Ride Hailing Apps Analysis

How should a ride-hailing platform match riders with drivers? A marketplace deep-dive set in Indian cab markets.

What I did: Modeled rider–driver matchmaking dynamics — supply, demand, and their trade-offs — and packaged it as a complete case-study deck.

Problem — Matching a rider to a cab looks simple. It's really a three-way trade-off between rider wait time, driver earnings, and platform efficiency.

Approach — Broke the marketplace into its matchmaking levers, mapped rider and driver scenarios, and built the reasoning into a structured case study.

What it shows — Marketplace thinking, and structuring an ambiguous problem end to end.

Currency Design Breakdown

What would the ₹ note look like if designed today? A research-led redesign of the ₹10, ₹20 and ₹100.

What I did: Benchmarked five global currencies and ISO/IMF standards, defined a must-have information hierarchy, and iterated bilingual, accessibility-first note designs.

Problem — A currency note is a product for literally everyone: sighted or not, any language, human or machine reader — plus counterfeiters trying to break it.

Approach — Studied the Euro, USD, Swiss Franc, Dirham and Krone; sorted every element into must-have / good-to-have / use-case-based; explored orientation, colour coding and security features across drafts.

What it shows — Research rigour, and designing under hard constraints: security, accessibility, culture.

Case study

Boarding Pass Design Analysis

One boarding pass that works for passengers, security and gate staff — layovers included.

What I did: Classified every field as static vs dynamic, re-weighted the hierarchy per stakeholder, and iterated five layouts including a connecting-flight format.

Problem — A boarding pass is one static artifact read by three different users, carrying information that keeps changing after it's printed.

Approach — Split what's known at issuance from what isn't, pushed live info (gate, status) to a QR layer, and sized type by decision priority.

What it shows — Information architecture and stakeholder empathy, on a product everyone has held.

Exploration

Elevator Call Buttons, Fixed

Why does everyone press the wrong elevator button? An interface rebuilt around real mental models.

What I did: Mapped the mental-model failure, specced product essentials for single- and multi-lift systems, and designed feedback-rich call interfaces backed by FIFO controller logic.

Problem — Call buttons ask for your direction; people think they're summoning the lift. The interface and the mental model disagree.

Approach — Separated user intent from system control; added position, status and wait-time feedback; handled downtime, hospitals and priority access as edge cases.

What it shows — Systems thinking: spec, algorithm and interface designed as one product.
