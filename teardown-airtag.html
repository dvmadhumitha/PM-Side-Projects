How do strangers’ phones find your keys without anyone knowing?

An AirTag in five layers, face to steel back. The decision: how to find a lost bag without letting the same network follow a person.

Best for
  1. Finding a checked bag that went missingAny passing iPhone reports where it is, so the bag is on your map before the airline admits it is lost.
  2. Finding the keys somewhere in the flatMake it beep, 50% louder than the first one, or follow Precision Finding to the cushion; the network never comes into it.
  3. Getting it handed back by a strangerWhoever finds it holds any phone against the tag and reads your Lost Mode message. No app for them, no fee.

Less suited toAndroid owners and bike thieves: it is iPhone only, and it warns whoever carries it, by design.

Who uses it
  • The frequent flyerDrops one in every checked bag. Wants to see it on the carousel before the airline admits it is lost.
  • The keys loserMisplaces things at home, not across town. Wants the speaker and Precision Finding, and never thinks about the network.
  • The bike ownerHides a tag in the frame as a cheap alarm. Wants the thief found before the thief is warned, and the system is not built for that.
  • The person who never bought oneFinds an unknown tag riding in their bag. Wants a clear warning, a way to make it beep and a way to switch it off.

    Encrypt before it leaves the phone

    Locking the report on the finder’s device is what lets the whole flow be anonymous. It also means no debugging from the middle: when finding fails, nobody at Apple can see why.

    Blindness is a promise with costs

    Apple cannot look a tag up for you or measure how well finding works. A PM gives up the usual analytics to keep the promise, and must argue for that trade in every review.

    Lose your devices and nobody can help

    The private key lives only on the owner’s devices, so there is no recovery desk. The failure mode is a user who expects Apple to find things, and the fix is expectation setting, not a server.

    Your second user never bought anything

Fig. 3 · The system behind a found AirTag
  1. The tag

    It calls out a code that keeps changing.

    • Bluetooth beaconSends a public key that changes about every 15 minutes, so no one can follow a single code.
    • Rotating keysEach key comes from a secret shared by your own devices. The secret never goes to Apple.
    The tag is a beacon, nothing more

    No GPS, no SIM, no model, a coin cell. Every clever part sits elsewhere, so the tag stays at $29 and the roadmap lives in iOS. Hardware generations change range and loudness, not the system.

    finds per tag · battery swaps a year
  2. Any passer by

    A stranger’s iPhone quietly helps.

    • Encrypt to the keyThe passing device locks its own location with the tag’s public key, so only the owner can open it.
    • Anonymous uploadThe report travels to Apple with no account or identity attached.
    The helper must never notice helping

    The report costs a stranger battery and data for someone they will never meet. Keep it invisible and the network scales to a billion devices; make it felt and people would want a switch. Silence is the design.

    battery cost per relayed report
  3. Apple’s servers

    A locked box Apple cannot open.

    • Report storeHolds the encrypted reports. Apple keeps nothing that links the finder to the owner.
    A store nobody can query

    Apple holds encrypted reports and no link between finder and owner. That keeps the privacy promise, and it rules out the dashboards a PM would normally want. Retention and abuse handling must be designed without ever reading the contents.

    reports stored per tag · retention period
  4. Your iPhone

    Only you hold the key that opens the reports.

    • Private keySynced only among your own devices, end to end encrypted, through iCloud Keychain.
    • Find My appFetches the locked reports, opens them and puts the tag on the map.
    The owner’s phone is the only decoder

    Keys sync through iCloud Keychain among your own devices, so the experience depends on the owner keeping at least one. The app is where the whole invisible system finally becomes a dot on a map, and gets judged.

    lost reports closed as found
In plain words

Like a message in a bottle written in a code only you can read. Strangers who find it pass it along without opening it, and the post office that carries it cannot read it either.

Design choices, and what they cost
  1. Change the code every 15 minutesA fixed code would let anyone follow a tag around. Rotating keys stop that. The cost: Apple cannot look a tag up for you; only your own devices can.
  2. Borrow a billion devicesThe tag needs no GPS or SIM; passing Apple devices do the work. The cost, as ByteByteGo notes: where Apple devices are few, updates are rare and less precise.

How it grew
The spike
AirTag went on sale on 30 April 2021 into a crowd of 1.65 billion active Apple devices, counted that January. A year later it was 1.8 billion, 300 million up on two years before.
What drove it
Not the tag. The crowd grows with iPhone sales, and Apple opened it in April 2021 to third party items under the Find My accessory program, so every new device finds more things.
How they iterated
December 2021: Tracker Detect for Android, so anyone could scan for a tag. May 2024: unwanted tracking alerts shared with Google on both platforms. January 2026: a second AirTag, longer range and louder speaker.

Apple active devices in millions, as announced each January, 2016 to 2026; the 2021 figure via MacTrast, the 2022 figure via 9to5Mac.

In plain words
A lost property box anyone can post a note into, and only the owner can open.
One promise owed to three people

The box image makes a promise to three people at once: the owner gets the item back, the finder stays anonymous, and nobody else learns anything. A feature that helps one by exposing another, however useful, breaks the product.

items recovered · privacy complaints
It optimises
Find the item without revealing the owner or the finder.
Privacy is the floor, speed the target

Speed of a find improves with more reports and finer location, and both reveal more about where people go. Hold anonymity fixed as a floor, then push time to locate as far as that floor allows.

time to locate · location error in metres
The dial
How often its identity changes: faster protects owners, slower helps spot a tag that follows someone.
A rate set with advocates, not alone

No single metric settles it, because the harms fall on different people. The PM sets the rate with safety advocates and legal, publishes the reasoning, and checks it in staged trials where a tag is planted on a volunteer.

planted tags flagged in trials · owners traced through their tag

The phone network paid for the tag

AirTag launched onto a crowd already built for phones, so the tag itself could stay simple and cheap. The reusable pattern: build the network for the product people already carry, then sell small endpoints that ride on it.

The edge
A $29 beacon, found by a billion devices that learn nothing.
  • The public key changes about every 15 minutes, from a secret only your devices share, so no single code can be followed.
  • A passing iPhone encrypts its own location to that key and uploads it anonymously; Apple stores reports it cannot read or trace.
  • Tile’s network is smaller; Chipolo and Pebblebee ride this one. The edge is the crowd, and it thins where iPhones are few.

The PM lens

“How would you design a product that protects user privacy by default?”

Apple PM interview · accessibility and privacy · Leland, Apple interview guide, Sep 2025
What the round tests

Whether privacy shows up in the architecture, not in a settings page.

  1. Make the company unable to know.Find My locks each location report so Apple cannot read it and does not know who found the tag. Privacy that does not rest on a promise.
  2. Rotate anything that identifies.The tag’s key changes about every 15 minutes, so no single signal can be followed.
  3. Protect people who never bought it.Someone with a tag hidden on them never chose this product. Warnings for them shipped on iOS and Android on 13 May 2024.
If this goes wrong

The network that finds a lost bag can follow a person. Privacy for the owner is not the same as safety for everyone else.

The decision inside

Find the bag without finding the finder.

  1. StopA lost bag, found by phones that belong to strangers.Nobody opened an app.
  2. NoticeThe network that finds a bag can just as easily follow a person.The owner and the person carrying the tag are not the same.
  3. TradeoffFind things fast, or make sure nobody is followed.Reach, or restraint.
  4. DecideKeys that change every 15 minutes, reports Apple cannot read, and since May 2024 warnings on iOS and Android for anyone an unknown tag travels with.What it cost: a tracker that has to tell strangers it is there.
