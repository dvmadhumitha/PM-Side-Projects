How does Duolingo know what you are about to forget?

One lesson, from the exercise you see to the models behind it. The decision turns on practising what you are about to forget.

Best for
  1. Keeping a school language aliveSomeone losing the Spanish they once learnt. Words come back just before they fade; Babbel would start them on a full course.
  2. Starting a language before a tripA beginner with a date in mind. Short lessons build the basics, and the streak carries them through the days they would skip.
  3. Practising speaking without a classA learner too shy for a classroom. Max adds role plays and a video call with an AI character; a tutor on italki still corrects better.

Less suited toreaching fluent conversation on its own; it builds vocabulary and habit, and a tutor or time abroad does the rest.

Who uses it
  • The streak keeperOpens the app daily, some days only to save the streak. Wants the day counted with the least friction.
  • The travellerHas a trip booked and a few months. Wants enough to order, ask the way and apologise.
  • The serious learnerStudies for an exam or a move. Wants proof of progress, and may sit the Duolingo English Test.
  • The parentSets up Duolingo ABC or maths for a child. Wants learning that feels like play and stays safe.

Part 1 · The lesson

The lesson: short, and logged answer by answer.

A lesson is a run of short exercises: translate a sentence, tap the word you hear, say a line aloud. Each answer is logged, right or wrong, with how long it took, and feeds everything below.

Sources · IEEE Spectrum · Lenny’s Newsletter

PM angleA lesson lasts a few minutes on purpose. Short is easy to start, and easy to repeat tomorrow.

Part 2 · The memory

The memory: a forecast of what you are about to forget.

Duolingo gives each word you know a half life: how long until your chance of recalling it falls to one half. Half life regression, published in 2016, learns those half lives from practice histories, and words near theirs come back.

Sources · ACL Anthology · Duolingo on GitHub

PM anglePractise what is fading, not what you just learnt. Review that comes too early wastes the few minutes a learner gives.

Part 3 · The difficulty

The difficulty: each exercise sized to you.

A model called Birdbrain estimates how hard each exercise is and how able each learner is, and predicts the chance you will get it right. Lessons are built to land hard enough to teach and easy enough to finish.

Source · IEEE Spectrum

PM angleToo easy feels pointless and too hard feels like failure. The band in between is a product choice, not a model output.

Part 4 · The habit loop

The habit loop: a streak to keep, a league to climb.

The streak counts days in a row with at least one lesson, and a streak freeze covers a missed day. Leagues put learners into weekly tables; the top of each table moves up a league.

Source · Lenny’s Newsletter

PM angleThe streak turns learning into something you can lose. That is why it works, and why it can feel like guilt.

Part 5 · The experiments

The experiments: every change is a test.

Changes go to a share of learners before release and are judged on whether people come back. Even which reminder to send is chosen by an algorithm that learns which messages still work.

Sources · Lenny’s Newsletter · Duolingo Research, at KDD

PM angleTests find what brings people back. They cannot say whether anyone learnt, unless learning is measured too.

Fig. 2 · From your history to today’s lesson
  1. 1LogEvery answer, right or wrong, with the time it took.the lesson
    The log is what the models learn from

    Both models learn only from what learners answer, so an exercise type that logs poorly teaches the system nothing. A PM who adds a new exercise format owns its labels too: what counts as right, and how partial credit is scored.

    answers logged per lesson · exercises with unclear grading
  2. 2ForecastEach word’s half life says how fast it is fading for you.what you will forget
    Review timing is a promise about memory

    Bring a word back too early and the learner spends minutes on what they know; too late and it is gone, which feels like failure. The forecast is worth only what recall days later says, so measure it then, not in the same session.

    recall on first sight after a gap · review share of lesson time
  3. 3PredictBirdbrain gives the chance you get each exercise right.hard, but not too hard
    The target band is the product

    Set the predicted chance of success high and lessons feel easy and get finished; set it low and learners learn more and quit more. Engineering supplies the predictions. The PM and the learning scientists pick the band and test it against completion and recall.

    lesson completion rate · accuracy per exercise
  4. 4BuildA lesson mixes new material with words due for review.today’s lesson
    Mixing hides review inside progress

    Learners like new words and resent going backwards, so review rides inside lessons that still move forward. The cost is that progress overstates learning. A PM watches the gap between how far a learner is on the path and what they can recall.

    path units completed · recall of earlier units
  5. 5RemindA reminder near your usual hour, with the streak on the line.come back tomorrow
    Reminders wear out with use

    Each notification spends a little attention, and the same message sent daily stops working. Duolingo published a bandit that rests a message after use so it can recover. The PM sets the ceiling on how many reminders a learner gets.

    opens per reminder sent · notifications switched off
  6. 6TestEvery change goes to a share of learners first.A/B test
    Tests answer the question you set them

    Most tests are judged on whether learners return, because that shows within days; learning shows in weeks. Without a learning metric beside retention, the test system keeps shipping whatever brings people back, whether or not they learn.

    next day retention · recall after a gap, by test group

Step 3 is the heart of it: a lesson is a forecast of what you can just about do.

Fig. 3 · The learning system, as Duolingo’s researchers describe it
  1. Your app

    Runs the lesson and logs every answer.

    • Duolingo appAnswers, response times and mistakes become the training data for both models.
    The app decides what counts as a mistake

    Typos, accents and word order are graded in the app, and each grading rule changes the labels both models train on. A PM who loosens grading to feel kinder also shifts every forecast downstream, so grading changes go past the learning scientists first.

    answers accepted with typos · grading complaints
  2. Learner model

    Remembers what you know and how fast you forget.

    • Half life regressionOne half life per word per learner, learnt from practice histories across all learners.
    • BirdbrainEstimates exercise difficulty and learner ability together, updated after every answer.
    Two models, two questions

    Half life regression asks when you will forget a word; Birdbrain asks whether you can do this exercise now. They can disagree: a word may be fading while its exercise looks easy. The PM decides which wins when lesson slots run short.

    review items per lesson · predicted against actual accuracy
  3. Lesson builder

    Picks the exercises for the next lesson.

    • SelectionChooses exercises whose predicted chance of success sits in a target band.
    • Review mixSlots in words near their half life beside new material.
    Policy lives in the builder

    The models give predictions; the builder turns them into a lesson under rules: how many new words, how much review, how hard. Rules change faster than models, which makes this layer the PM’s quickest lever and the easiest to overtune.

    new items per lesson · lessons abandoned midway
  4. Habit and tests

    Brings you back, and checks every change.

    • Reminders and testsA bandit picks which reminder to send; every change runs as an A/B test first.
    Motivation sits on top of learning

    Streaks, leagues and reminders bring the learner back to the lesson the models built. They are tuned on retention, which reads quickly. The PM keeps a learning metric in every launch review so motivation does not outgrow the teaching.

    current user retention · recall after a gap
In plain words

Like a patient tutor with a box of flash cards. She keeps a pile for each pupil, brings back the cards you are about to lose, and picks questions you will just about get right.

Design choices, and what they cost
  1. A forgetting curve for every wordA half life per word per learner lets review be timed word by word. The cost: words learnt together are treated as if they fade apart.
  2. Score the exercise before it is shownPredicting success for each candidate lets a lesson be assembled to a target. The cost: the target is a judgement, and the model cannot say what it should be.

The spike
Daily users rose about 65% in 2023, from 16.3 million to 26.9 million at year end, and another 51% in 2024.
What drove it
Our reading: habit features built since 2018 compounding, chiefly leagues and streak protection, plus the learning path of late 2022 and an owl people shared on social media.
How they iterated
Around 2018, leagues: weekly tables that lifted lesson time in tests. 2022, the path, one route instead of a tree of choices. March 2023, Max with GPT 4. 2025, chess, and 148 new courses made with generative AI.

What sets it apart
The algorithm · Half life
In plain words
A teacher who quizzes you on a word just before you would have forgotten it.
Your few minutes go where they count

The promise is that every review is one you needed. It breaks two ways: lessons full of words you know cold, which feels like a chore, or old words lost while new ones pile up, which feels like failure.

reviews of words already known · recall on first sight after a gap
It optimises
The chance you still recall each word, for the fewest reviews.
Recall and pace pull against each other

More review lifts recall and slows the path; less review speeds the path and lets words slip. Learners feel the path, not the recall. A PM sets a floor on each and judges every change against both.

recall after a gap · path units a week
The dial
How low recall may fall before a word comes back.
The threshold is a learning call with a growth cost

Learning scientists own the threshold, with the PM in the room because it changes how lessons feel. Test it as an A/B on recall a week later and on next day return, never on lesson accuracy, which rises whenever review comes sooner.

recall a week later · next day return
Same idea
Anki and SuperMemo: flash cards that return as you are about to forget them.
The schedule is common; the habit is not

Anki shows its schedule and leaves the motivation to you, and keeps a smaller, devoted audience. Duolingo hides the schedule inside a game. What carries over: spacing is solved, so the product question is what makes people turn up.

days practised a week · review share of lessons
The edge
A tutor’s schedule inside a game people open daily.
  • Half life regression: one forgetting curve per word per learner, so review lands just before a word fades.
  • Birdbrain predicts your chance of success on each exercise, and lessons are built to a target band.
  • Anki schedules as well and Babbel teaches as well. The edge is thin on method and wide on habit: a streak people protect daily.

The PM lens

“Daily users are up, but some learners say Duolingo never taught them to speak. What would you change, and how would you know it worked?”

Practice prompt in the style of a consumer PM product sense round · written for this page
What the round tests

Whether you can tell a habit metric from a learning outcome, and move one without breaking the other.

  1. Pick one segment and its pain.Learners with long streaks who still cannot hold a conversation. Their pain: practice that feels like progress and is not.
  2. Use the levers the system has.For this group, lower the success band on speaking exercises, and bring fading words back before new ones. No new screen needed.
  3. Judge it beyond daily users.Measure recall after a gap and a short monthly speaking check, beside retention. Fewer lessons a day is acceptable if recall rises.
If this goes wrong

Make lessons harder too fast and streaks break. A learner who loses a long streak may not come back at all.

The decision inside

Practise what you are about to forget, not what comes next.

  1. StopA word you learnt last month comes back in today’s lesson.A new word waits for tomorrow.
  2. NoticeFixed review schedules give everyone the same gaps.Some words fade in days, others hold for months.
  3. TradeoffNew material, which feels like progress, or review, which makes it stick.Moving forward, or remembering.
  4. DecideModel a half life for each word and each learner, bring words back near it, and publish the model in 2016.What it cost: review feels like going backwards, so it has to ride inside lessons that still feel new.
