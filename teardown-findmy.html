How does a stolen iPhone stay findable, even switched off?

An iPhone in five parts, lock screen to account. The decision: tie every phone to its owner, even at a cost to resale.

Best for
  1. Keeping a snatched phone findable when offThe thief switches it off at the crossing; it keeps calling out for up to 24 hours and passing iPhones relay it.
  2. Making a stolen phone worthless to resellWiped or not, it will not set up without your Apple Account; robberies fell 38% in San Francisco once that held.
  3. Getting a phone back from a taxiMark it lost from another device: it locks, shows a number to call, suspends your cards and keeps reporting where it is.

Less suited tothe second hand buyer: a seller who forgot to remove Activation Lock leaves them a phone that will not set up.

Who uses it
  • The commuter in a big cityPhone snatched from a hand at a crossing. Wants it back, or wants it worthless to the thief within the hour.
  • The forgetful ownerLeaves the phone in taxis and cafés. Wants Lost Mode and a number on the screen, never a police report.
  • The parent with a family groupShares locations across the household. Wants to see a teenager’s phone on the map and lock it from their own.
  • The second hand buyerPays for a used iPhone from a stranger. Wants to know before paying that it is not still locked to someone else’s account.

Part 1 · The lock screen

A message to whoever finds it.

Mark the phone as lost and it locks, shows your message and a number to call, suspends its payment cards and keeps reporting where it is.

Source · Apple Support

PM angleThe finder is a user too. A clear number on the screen is the fastest way home for most lost phones.

Part 2 · The radio

It keeps calling out, even offline.

With no Wi Fi or mobile signal, the phone sends a Bluetooth beacon. Any Apple device passing by reports it with its own location, locked so that only you can read it.

Source · TidBITS

PM angleEvery passing iPhone joins the search, and none of them knows it helped.

Part 3 · The power reserve

Findable after it is switched off.

From iPhone 11 on iOS 15, a phone that is switched off can still be found for up to 24 hours. At its lowest charge, it keeps reporting for up to five hours.

Source · Macworld

PM angleThieves switch phones off first. Designing for that moment is the whole feature.

Part 4 · The lock

The phone belongs to an account.

Since iOS 7 in 2013, a wiped iPhone cannot be set up again without its owner’s Apple Account. In the six months after, iPhone robberies fell 38% in San Francisco and 24% in London.

Source · TechCrunch

PM angleMake the stolen thing worthless and fewer people steal it. The best theft feature works on the thief, not the owner.

Part 5 · The account

Slowed down away from home.

Stolen Device Protection, from iOS 17.3, asks for Face ID with no passcode fallback away from familiar places, and waits an hour before an Apple Account password can change.

Source · Apple Support

PM angleA thief who watched you type your passcode still cannot take your account in a minute. The delay is the product.

    The thief’s first move is the off switch, so the feature only matters if it survives that. Power reserve turned a hard stop into a 24 hour window, and that window is the metric to defend.

    The same network that finds an AirTag

    A snatched phone is found the way a lost bag is: by strangers’ devices. Coverage therefore follows iPhone density, and the reports are locked, so Apple cannot help a victim it cannot see.

  3. 3Marked lostYou lock it and leave a message.Lost Mode
    The owner has to act under stress

    Lost Mode needs a second device or a browser and a calm head, minutes after a theft. The failure mode is the owner who does not know the step exists; the fix is education before the theft, not after.

    phones marked lost within an hour of theft
  4. 4WipedThe thief erases it to resell it.Activation Lock holds
    Deterrence is the metric, not recovery

    Activation Lock earns its keep before any theft, by making resale pointless. Its proof is robberies falling in San Francisco and London, which no in app dashboard shows. A PM has to borrow police data to see the win.

    robberies in cities with and without it · locked phones offered second hand
  5. 5AccountThe thief tries your Apple Account.a one hour delay
    The hour is the dial you own

    Longer stops more thieves and strands more owners; shorter does the reverse. The delay bites only away from familiar places, which is the clever part, and also the part that fails when a home is misread as unfamiliar.

    support contacts about the delay · share with it switched on
  6. 6OutcomeYou find it, or it stays locked to you.worth little to a thief
    Two good endings, one of them invisible

    A phone that comes home is measurable. A theft that never happened because phones stopped being worth stealing is not, and it is the bigger result. Report both, and resist judging the feature on recoveries alone.

Step 4 is the deterrent: a phone that cannot be set up again is worth far less to anyone who took it.

Fig. 3 · The system behind a stolen iPhone that stays yours
  1. The phone

    It stays findable, and it stays tied to you.

    • Offline beaconWith no signal it calls out over Bluetooth for passing Apple devices to report, as an AirTag does.
    • Power reserveThe beacon outlives the off switch: iPhone 11 and later on iOS 15, for up to 24 hours.
    • Stolen Device ProtectionFrom iOS 17.3. Its rules bite only away from familiar places such as home and work, so most days nothing changes.
    Protection that costs nothing most days

    Everything on the phone is designed to be unfelt until a theft: the beacon in the background, the reserve after power off, rules that sleep at home. The moment any of it is felt daily, people turn it off.

    share of phones with protection switched on
  2. The Find My network

    Strangers’ devices pass on where it is.

    • Encrypted reportsOnly the owner’s devices can open them. Apple cannot.
    One network, two products, one privacy bill

    The same crowd finds phones and tags, so a weakness in one is a weakness in both. nRootTag showed almost any Bluetooth device could ride the network, and the fix lived in iOS, not in the thing being tracked.

    months from disclosure to patch
  3. Apple’s activation server

    The gate every iPhone passes at setup.

    • Activation LockOn when you sign in and turn on Find My. After an erase, setup stops until the phone gets an activation certificate, which needs the owner’s Apple Account or old passcode.
    The only step that lives on a server

    Everything else is encrypted away from Apple; this gate is deliberately Apple’s. It decides who may own a phone, so a wrong answer strands a legitimate second hand buyer. Legal and support belong in the room before any change.

    locked phones in second hand sales · unlock requests upheld
  4. You

    Lock it, message the finder, find it.

    • Lost ModeFrom another device or iCloud.com: lock it, show a number to call, suspend its payment cards.
    The owner’s tools must work from anywhere

    Lock, message, suspend cards: all from another device or a browser, because the owner no longer holds the phone. The fewer steps between panic and a locked phone, the better; each one is a place to lose someone.

    time from theft to Lost Mode
In plain words

Like a car with both a tracker and an immobiliser. The tracker keeps reporting where it is, even parked with the engine off; the immobiliser means whoever drove it away cannot start it again without your key.

Design choices, and what they cost
  1. Tie the phone to a personActivation Lock is checked by Apple during setup, so wiping the phone does not wipe the lock. The cost: second hand buyers are stuck if a seller forgot to remove it.
  2. Slow down the dangerous changesAn hour’s delay before account changes away from familiar places stops a thief who watched you type your passcode. The cost: the owner waits too, when away from home.

How it grew
The spike
Every active device became a relay in September 2019, when iOS 13 shipped offline finding. The crowd was 1.4 billion that January; it has added 100 to 200 million a year since, to 2.5 billion.
What drove it
No launch moment for the user. The network rides on iPhone, iPad and Mac sales, so Find My grows wherever Apple sells, and the 2019 change turned that base into coverage overnight.
How they iterated
April 2021: AirTag and third party items joined the network. September 2021: iOS 15 kept an iPhone findable when powered off or erased. January 2024: Stolen Device Protection delayed account changes away from home.

Apple active devices in millions, as announced each January, 2016 to 2026; the 2021 figure via MacTrast.

In plain words
A car that will not start without its owner’s key, wherever it is taken.
The account is the key

An immobiliser works in any garage in any country. The promise is the same: control stays with the owner’s account wherever the phone ends up. It breaks the moment a factory reset or a repair bench lets a phone start fresh.

phones restored without the owner
It optimises
The owner stays in control, even when the thief knows the passcode.
Every lock also risks locking out owners

Count thefts where the thief gained nothing, and count owners shut out of their own phone. Tighten the rules and the first rises with the second. A lost Apple Account password becomes a dead phone, so recovery is part of the objective.

thefts with no gain · owners locked out
The dial
How much the owner is slowed down away from home.
Security and support want opposite settings

Security engineers would set it high and support would set it low. The PM weighs both against how often owners are actually away from home, and tests by country and travel pattern before deciding whether it ships switched on.

owners slowed while travelling · opt outs after a trip
Same idea
AirTag: the same network, finding things instead of phones.
Design first for the person being tracked

AirTag showed that a finding network serves a second person who never chose it: the one being tracked. Any new Find My feature should be checked for that person first, before it is checked for the owner.

unwanted tracking alerts · misuse reports per feature
The edge
The lock survives a wipe, and the beacon survives the off switch.
  • Activation Lock is checked by Apple’s server at setup, so a wipe does not clear it; robberies fell 38% in San Francisco.
  • Power reserve keeps the beacon alive for up to 24 hours after power off, and passing iPhones relay it, encrypted to you.
  • Google’s Find Hub and Theft Detection Lock answer much of this. What is left is the hour’s delay away from familiar places.

The PM lens

“Take a recent update to an Apple product. How would you work out what it did for the people who use it, and for Apple against its competitors?”

Apple PM interview · analytics and experimentation · reported to PracHub, 13 August 2026
What the round tests

Whether you can judge a launch with evidence on both sides: users, and the market.

  1. Pick the update and its job.Stolen Device Protection, iOS 17.3: stop a thief who has watched you type your passcode from taking your account.
  2. Measure what it did for owners.Accounts taken over after a theft, before and after. Then the cost: owners stuck behind the hour long delay, and the support contacts it creates.
  3. Measure where it puts Apple.Google announced Theft Detection Lock for Android in May 2024. Compare the protections a buyer can see, and how often each is switched on.
If this goes wrong

Friction that protects can also lock out the owner. If the delay triggers at home, people turn the feature off, and then it protects no one.

The decision inside

Make a stolen phone worth less than the trouble.

  1. StopA phone snatched from a hand, switched off within seconds.The owner watches it vanish from the map.
  2. NoticeThieves want to resell the phone, and switch it off to hide it.The phone is the prize, and so is the account behind it.
  3. TradeoffKeep phones easy to reset and resell, or tie each one to its owner.Convenience for second hand buyers, or safety for owners.
  4. DecideTie every iPhone to its owner’s account, keep it findable when off, and slow down account changes away from home.What it cost: owners who forget their password can lock themselves out too.
