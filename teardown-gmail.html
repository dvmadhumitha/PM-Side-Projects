How does your inbox know an email is spam before you open it?

One email traced from the sender’s server to your inbox, a tab or Spam, and the choice Google made: filter harder on its own side, or make bulk senders prove who they are.

Best for
  1. Getting the one email that mattersA bank code, an offer letter, a changed flight. It lands in front of you, not under a pile of sales.
  2. Keeping scams out without lifting a fingerMail that pretends to be your bank is stopped, or shown with a red warning, before you click.
  3. Reading sales mail when you choose toNewsletters and offers wait in Promotions, so the inbox holds people, not campaigns.

Less suited toAnyone who must prove a message arrived, and senders mailing a list they bought; Gmail gives no receipt and no person to appeal to.

Who uses it
  • The everyday readerChecks mail on the phone between other things. Wants the bank code and the parcel note in front, the sales out of the way.
  • The shop with a newsletterMails its customers every week. Wants to reach the inbox, or at least Promotions, and never Spam.
  • The admin at a small firmRuns Workspace for the office. Wants mail from the firm’s domain trusted, and phishing kept away from the accounts team.
  • The one who lost an emailWaited a week for an offer letter that sat in Spam. Wants to trust that real mail is never hidden.

Part 1 · The sender

The sender: a server that can claim any name.

A shop’s mail server hands the message to Gmail’s over SMTP, a protocol written in 1982. The From line is text the sender types, and the protocol itself checks none of it.

Sources · IETF · Google Workspace Admin Help

PM angleEmail trusts everyone by design. Every check after this one is a patch on that choice.

Part 2 · The checks

The checks: three records that prove the domain.

SPF lists the servers allowed to send for a domain. DKIM signs each message with a key the domain publishes. DMARC says what to do when they fail. Since February 2024 Gmail asks for all three from anyone sending 5,000 or more emails a day to it.

Sources · Google Workspace Admin Help · Google

PM angleA pass proves who sent the mail, not that you want it. It gives the next step a name to judge.

Part 3 · The reputation

The reputation: yesterday’s mail judges today’s.

Gmail keeps a record for each sending domain and IP address, built from how its earlier mail was received. Senders see it in Postmaster Tools, and bulk senders must keep reported spam below 0.3%.

Sources · Google Postmaster Tools · Google Workspace Admin Help

PM angleA shop that mails a bought list damages every email it sends after, receipts included.

Part 4 · The classifier

The classifier: a score for every message.

Machine learning models weigh the words, the links and the sender, and links are checked against Safe Browsing. TensorFlow models added in 2019 blocked 100 million more spam messages a day.

Sources · Google Workspace Blog · Google Security Blog

PM angleEach Report spam and Not spam click is a label. Users train the filter for free.

Part 5 · The inbox

The inbox: Primary, a tab or Spam.

Mail that passes lands in Primary or in a tab, Social, Promotions, Updates or Forums, which arrived in May 2013. Spam goes to its own folder and is deleted after 30 days.

Sources · Gmail Help · Official Gmail Blog

PM anglePromotions is a third verdict between inbox and Spam. It made being wrong cheaper.

Fig. 2 · Send, check, sort
  1. 1SendThe sender’s server hands the mail to Gmail.SMTP
    The cheapest no is at the door

    Gmail can refuse a connection before reading a word, from a sender whose record is bad. Turning mail away here costs less than filtering it later, and tells the sender at once. The risk is a real sender bounced with no clue why.

    connections refused · mail accepted per sending IP
  2. 2VerifySPF, DKIM and DMARC checked against the domain’s records.pass or fail
    Authentication gives reputation a name

    An unsigned email is anonymous; a signed one belongs to a domain that can be judged over time. So the share of mail that arrives authenticated is the number that makes every later step accountable, and the one the 2024 rules moved.

    share of mail passing DMARC · spoofed mail caught
  3. 3WeighThe domain’s and the IP’s record looked up.the track record
    New senders start in doubt

    A domain with no history is judged harshly, which is why senders raise volume slowly. On the sending side a PM plans a launch around reputation, not around the campaign calendar, and one bad list can undo months.

    domain reputation · reported spam rate
  4. 4ScoreModels score the words, the links and the sender.spam or not
    Two errors, priced very differently

    Spam in the inbox annoys; a real email in Spam can cost a job or a payment. The threshold leans towards letting doubtful mail through with a warning, and the PM owns that lean, not the model.

    spam reaching the inbox · real mail sent to Spam
  5. 5SortInto Primary, a tab or Spam.one verdict
    The tab is a verdict too

    Promotions is not Spam, but it is read less. For a sender it is a quiet failure; for Gmail it is a place for mail that is wanted but not urgent, and where its own ads sit.

    mail opened by tab · moves from Promotions to Primary
  6. 6ReportYou mark it spam, or not spam.a label
    The reader is the labeller

    Report spam and Not spam are the clearest labels the classifier gets, and they feed the sender’s spam rate too. Hide the button and the model learns slower; make it too easy and people report newsletters they signed up for.

    reports per thousand emails · reports on mail people subscribed to

Step 6 feeds steps 3 and 4: what you do with today’s mail sets how tomorrow’s is judged.

Fig. 3 · The system behind one Gmail delivery
  1. The sender

    Where the email is written and sent from.

    • Sending serverThe mail service of a shop, a bank or a friend’s provider. It speaks SMTP to Gmail.
    • Domain recordsThe SPF list, the DKIM key and the DMARC policy, published by the sender’s domain.
    The sender owns half the checks

    Gmail can only verify what the sender publishes. So the 2024 rules were a product launch aimed at other companies’ IT teams, with help pages, a deadline and Postmaster Tools as the dashboard.

    bulk senders fully authenticated · senders using Postmaster Tools
  2. The gate

    Gmail checks who is knocking.

    • AuthenticationChecks SPF, DKIM and DMARC, so a forged bank address fails before anyone reads it.
    • Reputation storeThe history of each sending domain and IP address, kept from how its mail was received.
    A gate tuned for strangers, not friends

    Most mail comes from senders Gmail has seen many times, so the gate’s real job is the newcomer and the forger. Its errors fall on small senders with no history, which is where a PM looks for false rejections.

    mail rejected from new domains · forged mail caught at the gate
  3. The classifier

    Models decide spam or not.

    • Spam modelsScore each message on its words, links, sender and how similar mail was treated by others.
    • Safe BrowsingGoogle’s list of dangerous sites, also used by Chrome, checked against every link.
    The model changes faster than the rules

    Spammers adapt within days, so the filter is retrained constantly and a rule written by hand is out of date quickly. A PM here judges a model change on both errors at once, never on spam caught alone.

    spam caught · real mail marked as spam
  4. Your inbox

    Where the verdict lands, and where you answer it.

    • Tabs and foldersPrimary, the four tabs and Spam, each a different promise about when you will see it.
    • Your reportsReport spam, Not spam and unsubscribe. Each one goes back to the sender’s record and the models.
    The interface is the training data

    Where the Report spam button sits, and whether unsubscribe is offered instead, decides what the models learn. An unsubscribe tells Gmail the mail was wanted once; a spam report tells it the sender misbehaved. Both are worth having apart.

    unsubscribes against spam reports · Not spam clicks
In plain words

Like the post room of a large office. A guard checks the stamp and the return address, a clerk looks up whether that sender has caused trouble before, and a reader skims the letter before it goes to your desk. Your complaints go back to the clerk.

Design choices, and what they cost
  1. Prove the domain, not the personSPF, DKIM and DMARC tie mail to a domain. The cost: a spammer can register a fresh domain and pass all three, so the checks name the sender without judging the mail.
  2. Let readers labelReport spam teaches the filter at no cost to Google. The cost: people report mail they asked for, so senders are punished for being forgettable.
  3. A middle verdictTabs give doubtful mail a home that is not Spam. The cost: senders treat Promotions as a penalty and work to get out of it.

Free storage is the meter

When the 15 GB fills, new mail stops arriving, and that is the moment Google One sells more space. Photos often fill it, not mail. A PM watches how many people sit near the cap and how many upgrade there.

Where the users are

No published split. Google gives Gmail only as one world total, 1.5 bn active users in October 2018, never by country or region. The nearest proxy, Alphabet’s revenue by region for the third quarter of 2026, puts the United States at about 53%, Europe, the Middle East and Africa at 26%, Asia Pacific at 16% and the other Americas at 6%, which is where the ads are sold rather than where people read mail.

How it grew
The spike
2016. Gmail passed a billion monthly users in February, almost twelve years after launch. It reached 1.5 bn by October 2018, the last count Google gave.
What drove it
Storage, then Android. Gmail opened in 2004 with 1 GB when rivals offered a few megabytes, and from 2008 every Android phone asked for a Google account, which comes with a Gmail address.
How they iterated
Open to all, February 2007, after three years by invitation. Tabs, May 2013, moved sales mail away from people. Smart Compose, May 2018, offered the next words as you typed.

Points: Google I/O, June 2012 and May 2015; Google, February 2016; Alphabet’s third quarter 2018 call. Active users as Google counted them.

What sets it apart
The algorithm · Classify
In plain words
A reader who gives every email a spam score and sends anything past a line to Spam.
Never lose real mail, never show a scam

The promise has two sides, and they pull apart. A bank alert in Spam breaks it worse than a newsletter in Primary, because the reader never knows it happened and stops trusting the inbox.

real mail found in Spam · phishing that reached the inbox
It optimises
Catching spam while almost never hiding real mail.
Catch rate and false alarms move together

Move the line and more spam is caught, and more real mail is hidden. Track both on one chart, never one alone, and weigh a lost bank alert above a lost sales email.

spam caught · real mail marked as spam
The dial
Where the line sits, and how far one reader’s reports move it for that reader.
The filter team owns the line

A threshold change is tested on a held back slice of mail and judged on Not spam clicks, the clearest sign of a false alarm. Personal reports should move the line for one reader far more than for everyone.

Not spam clicks · reports per thousand emails
Same idea
A card network flagging a payment as fraud works the same way.
Learn from the correction

A blocked genuine purchase loses a sale; a hidden genuine email loses trust. Both systems improve fastest from the person’s answer, “this was me” or “not spam”, so make that answer one tap away.

false alarms per thousand · time to learn from a correction
The edge
The biggest inbox teaches one filter for everyone.
  • Every Report spam click across Gmail trains shared models, so a campaign seen in a few inboxes can be caught in the rest.
  • Links are checked against Safe Browsing, the same list of dangerous sites Chrome uses.
  • Since 2024 bulk senders must authenticate, offer one click unsubscribe and stay under a 0.3% spam rate, rules Gmail and Yahoo set together.
AI inside

  • 2024: Gemini arrived in Gmail to summarise threads and draft replies, first for paying users.

The PM lens

“How would you measure the success of Gmail’s spam filter?”

Google PM interview · metrics and execution · a widely shared practice question, not a dated report
What the round tests

Whether you can hold two errors at once and price them differently.

  1. Name both mistakes first.Spam that reaches the inbox, and real mail sent to Spam. Any single number can be gamed by moving the line.
  2. Price them differently.A missed bank alert costs more than a newsletter seen. Weigh false alarms by what the mail was, with Not spam clicks as the signal.
  3. Count the senders too.Honest senders who bounce off the filter leave quietly. Watch delivery for authenticated senders with few complaints.
If this goes wrong

Report only spam caught, push it ever higher, and bury a job offer, a doctor’s letter and a login code in a folder nobody opens.

Watch
Spam reaching the inbox · Not spam clicks · spam rate by sender
With
Spam and abuse engineering · trust and safety · sender support · Gmail ads
The decision inside

Make bulk senders prove who they are.

  1. StopA sale emailed to millions of inboxes from a domain with no proof it belongs to the shop.Anyone could pretend to be the bank.
  2. NoticeThe filter caught most of it, but spoofed and unwanted bulk mail kept the cost on readers.Unsubscribe links that did nothing, senders with no name to hold to account.
  3. TradeoffFilter harder on Gmail’s side, or put rules on senders and risk real mail bouncing.Gmail’s guesswork, or the senders’ work.
  4. DecideFrom February 2024, anyone sending 5,000 or more emails a day to Gmail must pass SPF, DKIM and DMARC, offer one click unsubscribe and keep reported spam under 0.3%.What it cost: every large sender had to fix its setup, Google phased enforcement in through 2024, and Yahoo set the same rules.
