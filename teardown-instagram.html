How does Instagram choose the first post you see?

One feed, from the post you see to the rules behind it. The decision turns on ranking by prediction instead of time.

Best for
  1. Filling ten spare minutes in a queueNo one in mind, a feed that knows what will hold you. Friends and strangers in one scroll; TikTok shows only strangers.
  2. Catching up on a friend’s weekendSomeone who follows people they know and little else. Your history with them is one of the four signals that matter most.
  3. Posting to be found by strangersA creator who lives by reach. Suggestions carry a post to people who do not follow you, in Explore and Reels.

Less suited toseeing every friend’s post in time order; Following does it, but never by default, and Snapchat puts friends first.

Who uses it
  • The friend followerFollows people they know and little else. Wants to see what friends posted before any suggestion.
  • The creatorPosts to be found. Lives by reach, and feels every ranking change as a verdict without a reason.
  • The scrollerOpens Reels with no one in mind. Wants a feed that knows what will hold them for the next few minutes.
  • The advertiserBuys the attention of the other three. Wants the same predictions pointed at a purchase.

Part 1 · The feed

The feed: ranked, not in time order.

Since 2016 the feed shows what Instagram predicts you will care about, not the newest post first. Following and Favorites feeds in time order arrived in 2022, but neither can be the default.

Sources · Nieman Lab · TechCrunch

PM angleOffering a choice but keeping the default is a decision too. Defaults decide what most people see.

Part 2 · The candidates

The candidates: friends, and strangers you might like.

Feed mixes posts from accounts you follow with suggestions from accounts you do not; Explore and Reels are mostly suggestions. It avoids long runs from one account.

Sources · Instagram · Mobile Ecosystem Forum

PM angleMosseri says messaging, Reels and recommendations drove most recent growth. Suggestions are now the engine, not the extra.

Part 3 · The signals

The signals: thousands, and four that matter most.

For Feed the most important are your activity, information about the post, information about the person who posted, and your history of interacting with them.

Source · Instagram

PM angleSignals about you come first. The same post can rank high for one person and low for the next.

Part 4 · The predictions

The predictions: what you are likely to do.

Instagram makes about a dozen predictions for each post. In Feed it weighs five most: whether you will spend a few seconds on it, comment, like, share or tap the profile photo.

Source · Instagram

PM angleEach prediction is a lever with a cost. Weight likes and you reward bait; weight time and you reward length.

Part 5 · The rules

The rules: two kinds of no.

Posts that break the Community Standards are removed. Posts that break the Recommendation Guidelines stay up, but are less likely to be suggested to people who do not follow the account.

Source · Instagram

PM angleNot recommending is quieter than removing, and harder to see. Creators feel it as reach that vanishes without a reason.

    How much room suggestions get beside friends is decided here, not by the model. It is the dial the lens segment feels most: a friend’s post missing from the candidates cannot be ranked back in.

    Your own activity is the loudest signal

    Because the feed learns mostly from what you already did, it amplifies habits, including ones you would not choose. A PM asks which signals are allowed, not only which predict well: seconds spent predicts well and says little about value.

    first week retention of new accounts
  3. 3PredictAbout a dozen chances: pause, comment, like, share, tap.five weigh most
    The weights are the product

    Five predictions, five weights, and every change to them is a different Instagram. Lift share and the feed fills with things made to be sent; lift profile taps and creators win. The PM owns the weights, with ranking engineering and integrity in the room.

    satisfaction survey score after each weight change
  4. 4RankPosts are ordered by those predictions.for you alone
    No two feeds agree, so plan to explain

    Two people see different orders and neither can check why. The cost arrives as support tickets, creator complaints and, in 2023, a public explanation from Mosseri. A PM budgets for explanation as a feature, not as a crisis response.

    why am I seeing this taps · creator reach complaints
  5. 5MixNo long runs from one account; suggestions spread out.variety
    Variety is a rule, not a prediction

    Left alone, predictions reward whatever worked last, so one account could fill the screen. The spacing rule overrides the model, which makes it the cheapest lever the lens proposes: space suggestions further apart for people who follow friends.

    distinct accounts per session · friends’ posts seen per session
  6. 6FilterPosts against the guidelines are held back.not suggested
    The quiet no needs its own metric

    Removal is counted and appealed; a demotion is neither, unless the PM builds that. The lever is cheap to pull and expensive in creator trust, so pair integrity with the creators team and put the guidelines where people can read them.

    posts demoted per 1,000 · creator appeals

Step 3 is the heart of it: the feed is a forecast of what you will do next.

Fig. 3 · The ranking system, as Meta’s engineers describe it for Explore
  1. Your app

    Asks for posts and reports what you do.

    • Instagram appLikes, comments, shares and seconds spent become the signals for next time.
    Every scroll is a training label

    What people do in the app becomes tomorrow’s ranking, which makes the client the start of the loop, not its end. A PM who changes a button here changes the data the model learns from, so the ranking team hears about instrumentation changes first.

    events logged per session · label drift after client releases
  2. Retrieval

    From billions of posts to a few thousand.

    • SourcesEach source picks hundreds of relevant items from a pool of billions.
    • Two TowersOne network describes you, another each post, as lists of numbers called embeddings, so posts close to you are found fast.
    Recall is where good posts die unseen

    Anything the sources miss is gone before any judge sees it, and no ranking metric will show it. A PM asks what share of friends’ posts even reach the candidate pool, because the lens pain starts here, not in the weights.

    friends’ posts in the candidate pool · retrieval recall
  3. Ranking

    A quick sort, then a careful one.

    • First stageA light model scores thousands of candidates.
    • Second stageA heavier model predicts what you will do with the best 100: like, comment, share, linger.
    Two models, two budgets, one cut to set

    The light model buys speed and the heavy one buys accuracy, and only 100 posts get the expensive look. Widen that cut and latency and compute both rise. The PM decides the number, with the cost of a slow feed on one side.

    feed load time · candidates reaching the second stage
  4. Final rules

    Variety and safety on top.

    • RerankingIntegrity filters and diversity rules, so one account cannot fill the screen.
    Policy sits above the model, and it should

    Integrity and diversity are hand written rules applied last, so they can change without waiting for the model. That makes this layer the PM’s fastest lever and the one most likely to be blamed when reach drops.

    posts reordered by rules · reach change after a rule
In plain words

Like a talent show with heats. A quick first round thins thousands of hopefuls; the judges look closely only at the best hundred; the producers then shuffle the final line so it is not all the same act.

Design choices, and what they cost
  1. Cheap models first, costly ones lastLight models handle thousands of candidates; the heavy one sees only the best 100. The cost: a good post dropped early never reaches the careful judge.
  2. Precompute what you canDescriptions of posts are calculated ahead and cached, so finding candidates is a quick search for near neighbours; models retrain every hour. The cost: brand new posts wait for the next refresh.

How it grew
The spike
500 million in June 2016 became one billion by June 2018: the second 500 million took two years, after the first 500 million had taken nearly six.
What drove it
Stories, launched 2 August 2016 as an open copy of Snapchat: posts that vanish in a day, so people who would never post a permanent photo posted daily. The ranked feed arrived that June.
How they iterated
June 2018, IGTV for long video, which few watched. August 2020, Reels against TikTok, short video ranked for strangers. 2022, the feed went full screen and friends pushed back, so Mosseri pulled it in July.

Points: Fortune, Dec 2014; TechCrunch, Jun 2016 and Jun 2018; Bloomberg on Meta’s third quarter 2022 call, Oct 2022; Mobile Ecosystem Forum, Oct 2025.

What sets it apart
The algorithm · Recommend
In plain words
An editor who reprints the paper for every reader, every time they open it.
Each reopening must earn its front page

A paper reprinted at each open promises something new every time, so reopening is the habit being built. It breaks when the second open in an hour shows the same top posts, or when a close friend’s big news never makes the front page.

posts seen twice across opens · opens a day
It optimises
Time that feels well spent, judged by its own forecasts of what you will do.
Minutes count only if nobody regrets them

Time spent is easy to raise and says nothing about whether it felt worthwhile. The opposing number is regret, visible when people set a daily limit, mute accounts or delete the app. Push minutes hard and that second number climbs.

minutes a day · daily limits set
The dial
How much room suggestions get beside friends.
The dial also sets who keeps posting

Every slot given to a suggestion is one a friend’s post did not get, and friends who get few likes post less. A week of testing will not show that. The PM moves the dial slowly and watches how often friends post.

posts per active account · likes on friends’ posts
Same idea
YouTube: candidates, then ranking, then rules. The same funnel.
The same funnel can change the product

On YouTube the same funnel shifted viewing from subscriptions to recommendations, and the product became a different one. Instagram faces the same drift with Reels. The lesson: the funnel ignores where posts come from, so the PM must say which product it builds.

share of viewing from accounts not followed · Reels share of time
The edge
Friends and strangers ranked in one feed, by what you will do.
  • Two Towers retrieval: one network describes you, another each post, as embeddings, so near neighbours are found among billions fast.
  • A light model scores thousands, a heavy one predicts what you will do with the best 100, then rules space accounts out.
  • The same funnel as YouTube; TikTok ranks suggestions only. The edge is thin: a candidate pool that still holds people you follow.

The PM lens

“Propose an improvement to the Instagram feed. Why is it worth doing, and how would you know it worked?”

Meta PM interview · product design and strategy · reported to PracHub, 6 April 2025
What the round tests

Whether you pick one segment and one pain, and judge success beyond engagement.

  1. Pick one segment and its pain.People who mostly follow friends now see a feed full of suggestions. Their pain: missing what friends posted.
  2. Use the levers the system has.For this group, weight your history with someone more, and space suggestions further apart. No new screen needed.
  3. Judge it beyond time spent.Instagram says people are more satisfied with a ranked feed, so ask them. Pair satisfaction surveys with friends’ posts seen and replies sent.
If this goes wrong

Push suggestions too hard and friends disappear from the feed. People move to messages and other apps to see them.

The decision inside

Rank what you will care about, not what came last.

  1. StopA friend’s news at the top, though it was posted hours ago.Brands that posted since sit below it.
  2. NoticeIn time order, people missed 70% of their feeds.The more accounts you follow, the more you miss.
  3. TradeoffTime order, which people understand, or a ranking they cannot see.Predictable, or relevant.
  4. DecideRank by predicted interest since 2016; add Following and Favorites in time order in 2022, but keep the ranked feed as the default.What it cost: people wonder why they see what they see, which is why Instagram explained its ranking in 2023.
