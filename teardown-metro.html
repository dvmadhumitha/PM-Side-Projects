How does a metro gate take your bank card without asking your bank?

Five stops from the tap to your bank, and why the gate decides in half a second without asking anyone.

Best for
  1. Riding in Milan on a foreign cardStraight off the train, no ticket to choose. Tap in and out with the same card; the day is added up tomorrow.
  2. Taking the odd trip with nothing loadedA single trip now and then. One tap costs €2.20 for 90 minutes, with no travel card to top up first.
  3. Riding all day at a capped fareMany trips, with no need to guess your day in advance. From the fourth trip you pay €7.60 and no more.

Less suited tothe everyday rider with a season pass, who never meets a surprise charge days later.

Who uses it
  • The touristIn Milan for a few days with a foreign card. Does not want to learn the ticket machine, and will not buy a pass.
  • The occasional riderLives in Milan and drives most days. Wants the odd trip to cost a single, with nothing to top up.
  • The forgetful tapperTaps in, then leaves with the crowd without tapping out. Wants the charge explained before they have to call.
  • The season ticket holderHas a pass and never taps a bank card. Wants the gate just as fast for them.

Part 2 · The gate’s computer

The gate’s computer: decides in half a second.

Contactless gates are built to decide in 500 milliseconds or less. It checks the card and a deny list, and lets you through without waiting for your bank.

Part 3 · The deny list

The deny list: cards that failed to pay.

When a charge is declined, the card goes on a list that is sent to every gate.

PM angleThe list is the only defence at the gate. If it reaches gates slowly, a bad card rides all day.

Part 4 · The day’s taps

The day’s taps: added up tomorrow.

The next day ATM, Milan’s transport company, adds up your trips and charges the cheapest fare. From the fourth trip in a day you pay the day ticket price, €7.60, and no more.

    Half a second is the whole spec

    Every feature proposed for the gate, a loyalty check or a fraud score, must fit inside the time already spent. The PM’s job is to say no to anything that asks the queue to wait.

  2. 2RideNo ticket exists yet.90 min a trip
    Nothing to show the inspector yet

    A rider stopped on the train has only a tap in the back office. Inspection has to read the card, not a ticket, and the rule for a rider whose bank will later decline has to be written in advance.

    inspections that cannot read a tap · riders stopped with a valid tap
  3. 3Tap outSame card; the trip is complete.metro only
    The missed tap out is the daily failure

    A trip with no tap out has no end, so the fare engine must assume one. That assumption, and the words beside the charge, decide whether the rider calls support. Agree it with operations before the first statement goes out.

    trips without a tap out · complaints per thousand charges
  4. 4Next dayATM adds up the day and applies the cheapest fare.capped at €7.60
    The cap sells the tap

    Capping gives up revenue from riders who would have bought singles and wins riders who feared overpaying. The PM should know which group is bigger, because the cap is the marketing, and the arithmetic has to hold.

    share of days that hit the cap · revenue per tapping rider against ticket buyers
  5. 5ChargeOne payment to your bank.up to 3 days later
    Two numbers for one day of travel

    A hold of €1 to €10 lands before the fare does, so the rider sees two numbers for one day of travel. Neither looks like a metro ticket. The PM owns the explanation even though the bank owns the statement.

    complaints about unrecognised charges · days from travel to charge
  6. 6If declinedThe card goes on the deny list; gates stop opening for it.sent to every gate
    Leaving the list matters as much as joining

    The list fails two ways, and only one is cheap. A slow list gives a bad card extra rides; a wrong entry shuts a paying rider out with no one to ask. Decide how a card leaves the list before how it joins.

    minutes from decline to every gate · cards wrongly refused

The gate opens before anyone has paid. The deny list in step 6 is what makes that safe.

Fig. 3 · The system behind a tap at the gate
  1. At the gate

    A tap, and the gate decides on its own.

    • Card or phoneSends a one time code, as at a shop.
    • Gate readerChecks the card and a deny list kept at the gate, then opens in 500 ms or less, before any bank replies.
    Offline by design, so updates are slow

    The gate answers on its own, which is why the queue keeps moving and why nothing it knows is fresher than the last list it received. Every rule change ships to 113 stations, not to one server.

    gate decision time · age of the list at the gate
  2. ATM’s back office

    Where taps become journeys and fares.

    • Tap storeCollects every tap in and tap out, from every station.
    • Fare engineThe next day, adds up your trips and applies the cheapest fare, capped at the day ticket.
    Fare rules live here, not at the gate

    A new cap, a new zone or a fare rise changes code in the back office only, and riders feel it the next morning. That is the quiet advantage over paper tickets: pricing becomes a software release, with everything that implies.

    taps unmatched to a journey · fares corrected after the fact
  3. The card rails

    The usual path to your bank, once a day.

    • One chargeSent to your bank like a shop payment, up to three days after travel.
    Someone else’s rails, someone else’s fees

    Each charge pays the acquiring bank and the card networks, so one charge a day instead of one per ride is also a fee decision. The PM negotiates the cap with the bank in the room, not after.

    fees per journey · declines per thousand charges
  4. The feedback loop

    A decline reaches every gate.

    • Deny listIf your bank declines, the card is added to the list and the list goes out to the gates.
    The loop closes days late by design

    A decline can only follow a charge, and the charge comes days after the ride. So the loop never catches the first trip; it limits the second. Judge it by how few bad cards ride twice, not by zero.

    bad cards that rode twice · unpaid rides per thousand
In plain words

Like a bar tab. The bartender pours first and settles up at the end of the night, keeping a list of people who never paid. The gate lets you through on trust, totals the day, and remembers cards that bounced.

Design choices, and what they cost
  1. Open first, ask the bank laterWaiting for a bank would jam the gates at rush hour. The cost: a card that will be declined still gets one ride.
  2. Add up the day, then chargeOne charge a day with the cheapest fare replaces a charge per ride. The cost: a holding charge of about €1 to €10 may appear, and the real charge comes days later.

Drivers short, and gates that need none

The tap takes work off ticket windows and machines, not off the drivers the network is short of. A payment feature frees money and people on the station side; it cannot run the bus. Say which problem it solves before claiming it.

staff per million journeys · ticket office transactions
Where the users are

No published split. ATM gives journeys as one network total, 695.5 m in 2025, with no split by line, zone or how the fare was paid. The nearest proxies are its own running counts of metro trips paid by card, 13 m by the end of 2019 and 47 m by April 2023, and its statement that more than 60% of tickets sold were already paperless.

How it grew
The spike
2022. Journeys rose from 422.5 m in 2021 to 569.8 m, a third more in a year, and 2023 added another hundred million. The network is still below the 820 m of 2019.
What drove it
Reopening, not the gate. Restrictions fell away through 2021 and 2022, offices and tourists came back, and journeys followed. The tap helped the newcomers, since a visitor with a foreign card needs no ticket machine.
How they iterated
Contactless on bus lines 56, 70 and 73 from December 2020, a trial on the surface. All 1,500 surface readers live in April 2023, a €12 m project. Paper tickets withdrawn from January 2026.

Points: ATM annual results 2020 to 2025, journeys on ATM’s network in Italy each year.

What sets it apart
The algorithm · Rules
In plain words
A club door with a list of banned names: no call to the bank, just a glance at the list.
Every rider rides on credit

The door admits anyone not on the list, so the operator lends each rider a ride until the bank pays. The promise is tap and walk. Anything that makes them think at the gate, like two cards in one wallet, breaks it.

taps rejected at the gate · card clash errors
It optimises
Never slow the queue.
Queue speed is bought with unpaid rides

Every check moved off the gate keeps people moving and lets a few bad cards through. Put both on one chart at peak hour: riders per gate per minute against fares never collected. A gain in one must be priced in the other.

riders per gate per minute · uncollected fares
The dial
How often the list refreshes: often costs more, rarely lets a bad card ride twice.
Refresh where the losses are

Engineering knows what each update costs; the PM weighs it against fares lost. Refresh busy interchanges more often than quiet line ends, then compare repeat rides by bad cards across the two groups.

repeat rides by listed cards · update cost per station
Same idea
A shop till asks the bank first; the gate asks afterwards.
Ask afterwards wherever waiting costs more

Motorway tolls, vending machines and sales on a plane make the same bet: let the customer through, then collect. The reusable rule: cap what one unknown card can spend before the bank answers, and know who carries the loss.

spend allowed before authorisation · loss per unknown card
The edge
The gate opens on its own, before any bank has answered.
  • The reader checks the card and a deny list kept at the gate, and decides in 500 ms or less, offline.
  • The fare engine adds up the day the next morning and charges the cheapest fare, capped at €7.60, in one payment.
  • A paper ticket or travel card is paid before you know your day; the model itself is London’s, and ATM follows it.

The PM lens

“Many people find it difficult to pay for public transportation. How would you approach solving this problem?”

Whiteboarding round · reported to Prepfully by a Ripple candidate, December 2024
What the round tests

Whether you reframe the problem before you design a screen.

  1. Remove the ticket, not the payment.The hard part is choosing the right ticket before you know your day. Tap and pay later moves the choice to after the ride.
  2. Cap the day so nobody overpays.In Milan, from the fourth trip of the day you pay the day ticket price, €7.60, and nothing more.
  3. Price the risk of opening first.The gate opens before the bank answers, so the operator carries one unpaid ride per bad card. A deny list that reaches every gate keeps it at one.
If this goes wrong

If the deny list reaches gates slowly, one bad card rides all day, and every rider who forgets to tap out becomes a complaint.

The decision inside

Open first, charge tomorrow.

  1. StopA gate that opens in half a second for a bank card.No ticket, no machine, no queue.
  2. NoticeThe bank cannot answer that fast, and waiting would mean queues at every gate.Speed and certainty pull in opposite directions.
  3. TradeoffAsk the bank first, or open first and charge later.Certainty, or flow.
  4. DecideCheck a deny list at the gate, add up the day the next morning, and charge the cheapest fare, capped at €7.60.What it cost: the operator carries the first unpaid ride of every bad card.
