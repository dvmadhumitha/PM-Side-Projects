How does an app name a song from a few noisy seconds in a bar?

The drawing follows one tap, from the listen button to a hash. The decision turns on how much of the sound to throw away.

Best for
  1. Naming the song playing in a barPhone held up over talk and glasses; a match comes from the few peaks that survive, before the song ends.
  2. Naming music under a film or advertThe song sits under dialogue on the television. One press from Control Center names it without pausing what you are watching.
  3. Building a playlist from the week’s findsTags made all week sync to a My Shazam Tracks playlist in Apple Music, ready to play at the weekend.

Less suited toa tune stuck in your head: there is nothing to match, and Google’s Hum to Search can.

Who uses it
  • The person in the barPhone up in the air for a song they will forget by morning. Wants a name in seconds, then back to the night.
  • The screen watcherHears a song under a film or an advert. Wants it named without pausing what they are watching.
  • The playlist builderTags all week and listens at the weekend. Wants the finds to land in Apple Music without any fuss.
  • The hummerHas the tune but not a recording to match. Shazam cannot help; Google’s Hum to Search can.

Part 4 · The peaks

The peaks: keep the brightest points only.

Only the strongest points survive, and loudness is thrown away. In heavy noise, a match can come from just 1 to 2% of them.

Source · Avery Wang, ISMIR

PM angleThrowing data away is the feature. Less to store, less for noise to spoil.

Part 5 · The hashes

The hashes: pairs of peaks become a lookup.

Each point is paired with nearby ones; two pitches and the gap between them make one 32 bit number. A true match is a run of time offsets that agree.

Source · Avery Wang, ISMIR

PM angleStorage is cheap and waiting is not. The trade was made on purpose.

Fig. 2 · From a noisy bar to a song title
  1. 1ListenA few seconds through the microphone.up to 15 s in 2003
    How long to listen is the product dial

    Seconds of listening buy confidence and cost patience, and the person is holding a phone up in a bar. The PM sets when to stop and answer, and should move it only with wrong answer rates in hand, not with a stopwatch alone.

    seconds to a match · wrong names per 1,000 searches
  2. 2PictureThe sound becomes a spectrogram.time × pitch
    Drawn on the phone, on purpose

    Turning sound into a picture before it leaves the phone means the recording never does. The PM gets a privacy story for free and pays in battery and in old phones that are slow to draw it; both belong in the launch review.

    time to fingerprint by phone model · battery per search
  3. 3PeaksOnly the brightest points are kept.loudness dropped
    Where the match fails, look here first

    If a genre or a venue matches badly, the peaks are the first suspect: a quiet acoustic track or a bass heavy room leaves fewer survivors. The PM asks for failure rates split by music and by noise type before touching anything downstream.

    no match rate by genre · peaks surviving in heavy noise
  4. 4PairsPeaks are paired into 32 bit hashes.two pitches, one gap
    The pairing rule is the storage dial

    Pair each peak with more neighbours and matches get surer while the index grows; pair with fewer and it shrinks but misses more. The 2003 trade was ten times the storage for ten thousand times the speed; know that ratio before asking for either.

    index size per song · lookups per match
  5. 5LookupEach hash is looked up in a sorted index of songs.5 to 500 ms, 2003
    Every song added makes the index heavier

    Each new release adds its pairs to an index that must stay sorted, so a bigger catalogue is a bigger index to keep sorted and served. The PM weighs breadth, obscure tracks and live versions, against the speed and spend of keeping it fast.

    lookup time on the slowest searches · songs never matched once
  6. 6Line upThe song whose time offsets agree wins.a diagonal line
    The diagonal is where confidence is decided

    How straight and how long a line must be before Shazam answers is a threshold, and thresholds are product decisions. Raise it and covers and remixes go unnamed; lower it and a confident wrong title appears. Date every change and watch both.

    no result rate · wrong titles reported by users

Step 4 is the trick: a pair of peaks is far rarer than one peak, so the lookup lands on the song fast.

Fig. 3 · The system behind a song search
  1. Your phone

    Listens, then makes a fingerprint.

    • MicrophoneA few seconds of the room, noise and all.
    • SignatureA lossy summary of the sound, at least ten times smaller than the audio, and impossible to turn back into it.
    The button moved, so the moment must too

    Control Center recognition and ShazamKit mean the matching no longer needs the app open. The PM gains reach and loses the moment: a song named from a button leaves nothing to open, so the Apple Music handoff has to happen right there.

    recognitions from Control Center · tags that reach Apple Music within the minute
  2. Shazam’s catalogue

    Fingerprints of much of the world’s music, in the cloud.

    • Hash indexEvery pair of peaks, kept as one 32 bit number and sorted so a lookup is fast.
    The catalogue is a licensing and coverage decision

    An index only names songs it was given, so coverage is set by deals with labels and by what gets ingested first. The PM watches what people search for and fail to find, by country, because an unmatched song is the product saying no.

    no match rate by country · days from release to indexed
  3. The match

    The song whose timing lines up wins.

    • Time offset checkFor each candidate song, the matching hashes are lined up in time; a straight diagonal means a match.
    Same test, different stakes at YouTube

    The same idea decides in Content ID whether an upload is someone else’s song, where a false match costs a creator money. For Shazam a false match costs a shrug. The tolerance a PM accepts should follow the stakes, not the maths.

    false matches per 1,000 · matches overturned by the user
  4. The answer

    A title, an artist, a link.

    • ResultThe song’s details come back to the app.
    The answer is the only screen that earns

    Shazam is free, so this screen is where it sells Apple Music: a title, an artist, and a link that should land in a playlist without a hunt through settings. The PM measures the seven days after, not the tap.

    trials started from the result screen · tags synced to a playlist
In plain words

Like recognising a friend’s handwriting from three words. You need only a few distinctive strokes in the right order, not the whole letter. Shazam keeps just the brightest points of a song and checks whether they line up in time.

Design choices, and what they cost
  1. Send a fingerprint, not a recordingOnly a small summary that cannot be turned back into sound leaves the phone. The cost: work on the phone, and a catalogue of signatures for every song.
  2. Pairs of peaks as keysHashing pairs of loud points makes matches survive noise and compression. The cost: a very large index to keep sorted.

Where the users are

No published split. Apple gives Shazam one worldwide figure, 300 m+ monthly users and 100 bn recognitions by November 2024, and nothing by country; the nearest proxy is Shazam’s own charts by country and city, which rank songs, not people.

How it grew
The spike
2021: a billion recognitions a month for the first time, Apple said in June. The running total went from 50 bn then to 70 bn by August 2022, 20 bn in fourteen months.
What drove it
Apple. It bought Shazam in September 2018, dropped the ads and built recognition into iOS itself, in Siri and Control Center, so a tap works without finding or opening an app.
How they iterated
Jun 2021: ShazamKit let other apps use the matcher. Sep 2023: Shazam became an option for the iPhone 15 Pro Action button, one press from the pocket. Nov 2024: 100 bn recognitions in all.

Points, a tag being one recognition: Shazam’s milestones of Aug 2012 and Sep 2013 as reported, Apple’s of Jun 2021 and Aug 2022, Music Business Worldwide for Nov 2024.

How they engage
  • ~37 msongs recognised a day, 2022 to 2024
    Daily recognitions have plateaued since 2021

    A billion a month in 2021 and about 1.1 bn a month since means the habit stopped growing once it lived inside iOS. The next gain has to come from new moments to tap, not new users. Watch recognitions by entry point.

    recognitions a day · share from Control Center and Siri
  • ~4recognitions a month per user
    Shazam is an occasional tool, by design

    A user names a few songs a month, when one catches them. Low frequency is not failure here, but it makes the moment after the match matter more than the count: the song saved, played in full, or added to a list.

    songs saved per match · plays in full after a match

Both are worked out from figures already on the page: 30 bn recognitions between August 2022 and November 2024, about 27 months, against 300 m+ monthly users. Shazam publishes neither figure itself, so read both as estimates.

In plain words
Knowing a friend by their walk, across a crowded square.
Sell recognition, not search

The analogy says the user brings no words at all. That sets the whole job: one tap, nothing to type, an answer before the song ends. Any feature that asks for input breaks the promise.

time from tap to title · taps abandoned before a result
It optimises
A right answer from a few noisy seconds.
Two numbers that pull against each other

Answer sooner and wrong titles creep in; wait longer and people give up. A PM sets a floor for each and tests every change against both, never one alone.

correct match rate · seconds to an answer
The dial
How long to listen: longer is surer, shorter feels like magic.
The listening window is a product call

Engineering can give the accuracy at each length. Only the PM can judge how long someone in a bar will hold a phone up. Test the window by setting, noise and phone, and date every change.

The PM lens

“How would you improve Apple Music for Gen Z users?”

Apple PM interview · product sense · Leland, Apple interview guide, Sep 2025
What the round tests

Whether you find the user’s real moment before the feature list.

  1. Find the moment of intent.A new song is often first heard somewhere else: a video, a shop, a bar. Shazam is where that intent shows up.
  2. Close the gap to listening.Found songs can already sync to a My Shazam Tracks playlist in Apple Music, but only if a setting is on. Make the first sync part of the first recognition.
  3. Count listens, not tags.A tagged song never played again is a dead end. Measure songs played within seven days of being found.
If this goes wrong

Push too hard and Shazam becomes an Apple Music advert. People who listen elsewhere stop opening it.

The decision inside

Throw most of the sound away.

  1. StopA song in a noisy bar, named in seconds.From a phone held up in the air.
  2. NoticeComparing the sound itself is slow and breaks in noise.Waves change with every speaker; peaks mostly do not.
  3. TradeoffStore more, or search faster.A bigger index, or a slower answer.
  4. DecideKeep only the peaks, pair them into hashes, and look the pairs up.What it cost: about 10 times the storage, for about 10,000 times the speed (Wang, 2003).
