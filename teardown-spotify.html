How does Spotify pick thirty songs you have never heard?

The drawing follows one play, app to taste profile. The decision turns on a short list each Monday, not an endless feed.

Best for
  1. Finding thirty new songs each MondayEvery play, skip and save feeds a taste profile; Discover Weekly returns thirty new songs, where your own records would only repeat.
  2. Listening on a plane or undergroundPremium keeps up to 10,000 songs on each of five devices, so the music keeps playing where a stream would stop.
  3. Music on the commute without a billThe free tier plays on the bus with ads and shuffle; most listeners never pay, and the ads help cover it.

Less suited toa brand new track nobody has played yet; it cannot reach Monday’s list, and SoundCloud is where it lives.

Who uses it
  • The free commuterListens on the bus with ads and shuffle. Wants music without a bill and will put up with the breaks.
  • The Premium regularPays for no ads, downloads and lossless. Wants the app to know their taste better than they do.
  • The family account holderOne subscription, several ears. Wants the children’s songs kept out of a parent’s profile and Monday’s playlist.
  • The talk listenerComes for podcasts and audiobooks and stays for hours. Wants one app for all of it, with their place kept.

Fig. 2 · From your listening to Monday’s playlist
  1. 1ListenYou play, skip, save and search.every action counts
    Not every action should weigh the same

    A skip inside 30 seconds and a save say opposite things; a play left running says little. The PM decides the weights, and that choice is the taste profile before any model runs.

    skips in the first 30 seconds · saves per listener
  2. 2ProfileThose actions update your taste profile.the strongest input
    A profile forgets only when told to

    Taste changes faster than a profile built on years of plays. The PM sets how quickly old listening fades and whether a shared account splits into people. Get it wrong and the children’s songs follow the parent for months.

    saves from new genres · share of picks drawn from recent listening
  3. 3CandidatesSongs played by people with similar taste are gathered.new to you
    Similar listeners decide what can be found

    Only songs someone like you has already played can enter the list, so a brand new track with no listeners cannot. The PM decides how much to seed from sound alone, which is the dial between safe matches and real discovery.

    candidates from similar sound · songs with few listeners that get saved
  4. 4RankCandidates are ordered for you, beside editors and business rules.Discovery Mode can lift a song
    The business dial needs a published limit

    A lifted song costs the listener a better match they never see. The PM owns how far Discovery Mode may move a song and how it is labelled, and should measure its skips against ordinary picks, because the loop will hide the cost otherwise.

    skips on lifted songs against ordinary picks · saves per playlist
  5. 5DeliverThirty songs land in Discover Weekly.every Monday
    The cap of thirty is the habit

    A short list on a fixed day is a ritual, and rituals are measured by who comes back next Monday, not by Monday’s plays. The PM resists adding songs; the 2025 genre buttons add thirty more only on request.

    listeners who open it two Mondays running · songs played from it in the week
  6. 6LearnWhat you skip or save feeds next week.the loop closes
    A weekly loop learns in batches of thirty

    Feedback arrives in batches, so a bad week is not corrected until the next one. The PM decides whether a skipped song is a miss or just a mood, and whether the genre buttons should teach faster than Monday does.

    saves that become repeat listens · weeks until a new taste appears

Step 4 is where business meets taste: Spotify says cost and monetisation may influence what it recommends.

Fig. 3 · The system behind a play and a playlist
  1. Your app

    Plays, skips and saves, online or off.

    • Spotify appStreams audio or plays downloads. Every action becomes an event: a small record of what happened.
    Offline plays still have to be counted

    A download played underground is a royalty and a signal that arrives late, when the phone reconnects. The PM owns what happens in the gap: which events must never be dropped, and how long the app keeps them before giving up.

    events arriving late · offline plays that never report
  2. Audio delivery

    Songs come from servers near you.

    • Content delivery networksSpotify rents them rather than building its own; audio has run through Fastly’s network.
    Renting delivery buys focus, loses the last hop

    The choice puts engineering into the event stream and the recommender rather than boxes in providers. The PM accepts that a bad evening on a rented network is still a Spotify outage, and wants more than one supplier to switch between.

    time to first second of audio · stalls by network supplier
  3. The event stream

    Every play is written down.

    • Cloud Pub/SubGoogle’s message service carries events from apps into storage: more than 8 million a second at peak in 2019.
    • Storage and warehouseCloud Storage keeps the data; BigQuery lets analysts ask questions of it.
    Losing an event means not paying someone

    Most products can drop a few analytics events; here each one is a royalty, so the pipe is built never to lose any, at a cost in complexity and delay. The PM decides which new events earn that guarantee and which can be sampled.

    events lost or duplicated · delay from play to warehouse
  4. What a play feeds

    Paying artists and learning taste.

    • Royalties and countsEndSong, the event sent when a song finishes, is used to pay royalties and to count users.
    • RecommendationsYour playlists, your taste profile and the catalogue feed Discover Weekly, every Monday.
    One event, two owners who want different things

    Finance needs EndSong exact and auditable; the recommender wants it fast and rich. When a PM changes what counts as a finished song, royalties and user counts move with it, so label relations and finance belong in that review.

    royalty disputes after a release · EndSong counts against reported users
In plain words

Like a jukebox that writes down every song it plays. The notebook pays the musicians at the end of the month and tells the owner which records to put in next week.

Design choices, and what they cost
  1. One stream for money and tasteThe same event that counts a play also pays rights holders. The cost: events can never be lost, at millions a second.
  2. Rent the delivery networkUnlike Netflix, Spotify uses commercial networks for audio. The cost: less control of the last hop, in exchange for no servers to run in every provider.
  3. A weekly batch, not a live feedThirty songs on Monday can be made in bulk. The cost: a new taste waits up to a week to show up in that playlist.

How it grew
The spike
2020: 74 m monthly users added in one year, 27%, a record at the time, to 345 m. 2023 then added 113 m, still the most in a single year.
What drove it
Lockdowns, and podcasts. From March 2020 listening moved from the commute to the kitchen, and Spotify signed Joe Rogan in May 2020 to make the free tier worth opening every day.
How they iterated
Feb 2021: more than 80 new markets, for the next billion listeners. Feb 2023: the AI DJ, so a tap keeps playing. Nov 2023: audiobooks inside Premium in the US, to lift hours.

What sets it apart
The algorithm · Recommend
In plain words
A friend who knows your records, and what people with your taste are playing.
Trust is earned by being right

A friend earns the right to suggest by being right most of the time, and says why. The PM’s promise is personal taste, not popularity. One run of chart hits, and the friend becomes a radio station.

share of picks outside the charts · picks played to the end
It optimises
More hours of listening, without the same ten songs on repeat.
Hours are easy to buy with repeats

The cheapest way to add hours is the songs people already love, which are rarely skipped. So hours alone drift towards repetition and a narrower taste. The PM pairs hours with a breadth measure and accepts a few more skips as the price.

hours per listener · distinct artists played a month
The dial
Familiar against new: how much discovery to mix in.
Each surface needs its own mix

A morning commute and a Monday discovery list want opposite answers, and no model knows which promise a surface makes. The PM sets the mix per surface, then tests each one separately, because one global setting averages two jobs into neither.

skip rate by surface · new artists saved by surface
Same idea
YouTube: the same two stage funnel, scored on watch time and on survey answers.
Behaviour alone flatters the model, so ask

YouTube learned that watch time alone rewards what people cannot stop doing, not what they value, so it began asking. For Spotify the lesson is cheap: a short question after a list catches the songs that filled hours but were never wanted.

The edge
One event stream that pays the artists and learns your taste.
  • Every finished song is an EndSong event, carried at millions a second and never lost; one record pays royalties and trains taste.
  • Candidates come from people with similar taste, then thirty are ranked into a Monday list, so the loop learns in weekly batches.
  • Apple Music is built into the iPhone and Amazon Music gave HD away first; Spotify’s edge is the profile, not the sound.

The PM lens

“Explain how Spotify personalises music recommendations, from signals to ranking.”

Spotify PM interview · machine learning · reported to PracHub, 9 August 2026
What the round tests

Whether you can explain a recommender in plain words, and say where a PM’s judgement enters it.

  1. Start with the signals.What you search, play, skip and save, plus your age, who you follow, your device and language. A song you abandon teaches as much as one you finish.
  2. Then candidates and ranking.Gather songs from similar listeners and similar sounds, then order them for you. Editors and business rules sit beside the model.
  3. Then the loop.Thirty picks a week means thirty chances to learn. Measure saves and repeat listens, not just plays.
If this goes wrong

Optimise for streams alone and the playlist drifts toward safe, familiar songs. Discovery dies quietly, one easy click at a time.

The decision inside

Deliver discovery as a weekly ritual, not an endless feed.

  1. StopA playlist that is new every Monday, and gone by the next.Thirty songs, no more.
  2. NoticeAn endless list of suggestions is easy to ignore.A small, fresh batch feels like a gift.
  3. TradeoffInfinite recommendations, or a short list on a fixed day.Volume, or habit.
  4. DecideThirty songs for each listener every Monday since 2015; genre buttons added in 2025 bring thirty more at a time.What it cost: many good matches never surface, because each week holds only thirty.
