How does a ₹20 scan reach a stall’s bank in seconds?

One scan traced from the counter to two banks, and the choice NPCI faces: keep every payment free, or charge the big ones.

Best for
  1. Paying a stall by scanning its stickerA ₹20 chai at a counter with a printed code. The stall pays no fee, so it will not ask for cash.
  2. Sending rent that has to land nowRent, fees or a deposit, from your account to theirs in seconds, with a name instead of an account number to type.
  3. Splitting a bill with a friendPay a friend by name, not account number. Nothing to load first, as a wallet needs, and no fee either way.

Less suited toAnyone without a phone or a signal, and any day the one switch stumbles; cash keeps working through both.

Who uses it
  • The chai drinkerPays ₹20 by scanning a code. Wants it free and instant, and never thinks about which bank is behind it.
  • The stall ownerA printed code and a phone. Wants the money in the account before the customer walks away, and no fee.
  • The sender of a big paymentRent, fees, a deposit. Wants a large transfer to land now, not after an hour’s hold.
  • The first time payerA new bank account and a phone. Wants it to work the first time and say plainly when it has not.

Part 1 · The QR code

The QR code: a payment address, not an account number.

The code at the counter holds a UPI ID, a name at a bank. The payee’s bank turns it into an account only when you pay, so the shop never shows its account number.

Part 2 · The app

The app: the front door, not the bank.

Apps like PhonePe and Google Pay pass your request to the banks. In September 2026 PhonePe carried 45.57% of UPI payments, Google Pay 32.23% and Paytm 8.1%.

Sources · MediaNama · Setu

PM angleA 30% cap on any one app is due from 31 December 2026. Two apps above 30% is a regulatory risk, not just a market share.

Part 3 · The PIN

The PIN: locked on the phone, checked by your bank.

A common library inside the app captures your UPI PIN and encrypts it. Only your bank checks it, against a salted hash, so neither the app nor NPCI ever reads it.

Sources · Setu · Google Pay Help

PM angleFor payments up to ₹1,000, UPI Lite skips the PIN entirely. Friction is a dial, set by the amount.

Part 4 · The switch

The switch: one router for every bank.

NPCI sits in the middle. It finds the payee’s bank from the UPI ID, asks your bank to debit and theirs to credit. Since June 2025 a payment must answer within 15 seconds, down from 30.

Sources · Setu · MediaNama

PM angleAfter outages in spring 2025, traced to banks overusing status checks, NPCI limited those calls. One rail means one outage reaches everyone.

Part 5 · The banks

The banks: your money moves now, the banks settle later.

Within seconds your bank debits you and the shop’s bank credits the shop. 703 banks were on UPI in the year to March 2026, from 44 in its first year.

Sources · Setu · YourStory

PM angleEvery bank speaks the same language. That, more than any one app, is why UPI spread.

Fig. 2 · Scan, PIN, paid
  1. 1ScanThe app reads the UPI ID from the code.name@bank
    A name on a sticker never needs reprinting

    Because the code holds a name, not an account, the stall keeps the same sticker when it changes banks, and the app never learns the account. The scan is a merchant’s whole onboarding, so time from camera to amount screen is the product here.

    scans that reach the PIN screen · time from scan to amount
  2. 2PINYou type it; the phone encrypts it.the app never reads it
    The screen you cannot redesign

    The PIN pad belongs to NPCI’s library, not to the app, so the step with the most abandonment is one an app PM cannot change. Her levers sit around it: UPI Lite below ₹1,000, and treating wrong PIN declines as a product signal.

    drop off at the PIN screen · wrong PIN declines
  3. 3RouteNPCI asks the payee’s bank which account it is.the address resolved
    The lookup is where the clock starts

    The address check is the hop UPI pays for privacy, and it spends part of the 15 second budget before any money moves. When a payee bank is slow, payments pile up here, so lookup time by bank is the first chart to read.

    lookup time by payee bank · timeouts before debit
  4. 4DebitYour bank checks the PIN and takes the money.seconds
    The bank says no, the app wears it

    Most declines are born here, and the customer sees only the app’s error screen. So an app PM publishes success rate by payer bank, splits wrong PIN from timeout, and takes the bank’s number to the bank rather than tuning her own screens.

    success rate by payer bank · technical declines apart from business ones
  5. 5CreditThe shop’s bank pays the shop.seconds
    Debited but not credited is the worst state

    A debit that lands and a credit that stalls leaves the customer paid and the stall unpaid, with both looking at the app. How fast the money is returned or completed decides whether a merchant keeps the sticker up.

    payments debited but not credited · time to reversal
  6. 6SettleThe banks square up with each other.later
    Nobody at the counter pays for this step

    The customer sees seconds; the banks carry the cost of squaring up, and the fee that would pay for it was deferred on 13 October. A PM on this rail owns the question of who funds reliability when the price is zero.

    cost per payment to the banks · share of payments above ₹2,000

Step 3 happens before any money moves, so a payment never leaves your account without a valid place to land.

Fig. 3 · The system behind one UPI payment
  1. Your phone

    Where you scan the code and type your PIN.

    • Payment appPhonePe, Google Pay or Paytm. Apps like these are called payment service providers, or PSPs.
    • Common libraryNPCI’s code inside every app. It captures the PIN and encrypts it, so the app never reads it.
    The app owns the glass, not the rail

    An app differentiates on everything around the payment: the scan, the contact list, the receipt, the cashback. The payment itself is identical in every app, which is why share can move fast, and why the 30% cap matters more than any feature.

    app share of payments · payments per active user
  2. The middle

    One exchange that every bank is wired to.

    • The app’s bankEach app works through a partner bank that passes its requests on.
    • NPCI switchThe central router: it finds which bank a UPI ID belongs to and tells each bank what to do.
    Shared rails need shared rules on load

    Every app reaches NPCI through a partner bank, so a slow partner bank looks like a slow UPI. After the spring 2025 outages the fix was a rule on status checks, not more servers: on a shared rail the lever is a limit on others.

    status checks per payment · switch response time by bank
  3. The two banks

    Yours pays out; the shop’s pays in.

    • The shop’s bankTurns name@bank into a real account.
    • Your bankChecks the PIN against a salted hash, a scrambled copy it keeps, and debits you.
    Two banks must both answer inside 15 seconds

    A payment is only as fast as the slower of two banks, neither of which the app or NPCI runs. Success rate by bank, published and compared, is how the ecosystem pressures the slow ones, since no PM can file a ticket against a bank.

    success rate by bank · bank response time at peak
  4. Later

    The banks square up.

    • SettlementPeople see the money move in seconds; the banks settle what they owe each other afterwards.
    Instant for people, deferred for banks

    The customer’s seconds exist because the banks agree to square up later on the switch’s record. Nothing here is visible to users until a tally disagrees, so accuracy of that record, not speed, is what this layer protects.

    settlement disputes between banks · time to reconcile
In plain words

Like a telephone exchange for money. Your app dials a name, not a number; the exchange looks up which bank answers to that name and connects the two banks for a few seconds. The banks tally up afterwards.

Design choices, and what they cost
  1. One switch for every bankEach bank connects once, to NPCI, instead of to hundreds of others. The cost: when the switch or a big bank struggles, everyone feels it, as in the spring 2025 outages.
  2. Addresses, not account numbersA UPI ID is turned into an account only when you pay, so account numbers stay private. The cost: one more lookup inside every payment.
  3. Only your bank sees the PINThe app captures it through NPCI’s library; your bank alone checks it. The cost: a PIN for every payment, which UPI Lite drops for payments up to ₹1,000.

How it grew
The spike
The year to March 2019: payments went from 0.9 bn to 5.4 bn, almost six fold, the steepest year on the line. The big absolute jumps came later, 46 bn to 131 bn by 2024.
What drove it
The apps, not the rail. Google launched Tez on 18 September 2017, Paytm added UPI that November, and cashbacks plus a printed code at the stall made the first payment cheap to try.
How they iterated
UPI 2.0, August 2018, added overdraft accounts and payment mandates. UPI 123Pay, March 2022, brought feature phones in without data. UPI Lite, September 2022, made small payments one tap, no PIN.

Points: years to March. NPCI figures as tabled in the Economic Survey 2023 to 2024 for 2018 to 2024, and the Ministry of Finance for 2026.

What sets it apart
The algorithm · Look up
In plain words
A switchboard operator who knows which bank every name belongs to.
Routing a name is the whole job

The operator holds no money and owns no customer; the promise is only that a name always reaches the right account. A stale entry, after someone closes or moves an account, breaks that promise for every person who pays the name.

lookups that find no account · payments returned by the payee bank
It optimises
An answer for every payment within seconds, even when a bank is slow.
Pending is worse than a clean failure

Success, failure and pending are three numbers, and pending hurts most: the payer is unsure and the stall keeps the chai. A PM drives pending toward zero first, even if a few more payments fail outright.

payments left pending · success rate
The dial
Timeouts and retries: wait longer and fewer payments fail, but people stare at a spinner.
One timeout must suit chai and rent

NPCI sets one limit for every payment, yet a chai buyer and a rent sender tolerate very different waits. Patience is a product judgement, not a server setting. A PM tests any change by amount and by bank, and watches abandonment, not only failures.

abandonment while waiting · success rate by timeout setting
Same idea
The internet’s address book, DNS, turns names into numbers the same way.
Names invite impostors, so prove the answer

DNS was built on trust and later had to add proof that an answer is genuine. A name based rail faces the same pressure: a handle that looks right can send money to a stranger. Show the payee’s registered name before the PIN.

payee name shown before the PIN · payments reported as misdirected
The edge
One switch, every bank, a name instead of an account, no fee.
  • A UPI ID becomes an account only when you pay, at the payee’s bank, so the stall never shows its account number.
  • Every app reaches one switch through a partner bank, 703 banks speak one language, and a payment must answer within 15 seconds.
  • Cards charge the shop and wallets need loading first; UPI debits the bank directly for nothing. Pix does the same, in Brazil.

Payers measure trust in failed payments

A payer judges UPI on whether money arrives and whether a mistake can be undone. They need quick refunds and a way to dispute a wrong payment. Their tension is with the banks’ fraud checks: every check added slows the payment they chose for speed.

MerchantsEvery customer able to pay them, for nothing.

Free for shops, paid by someone else

Merchants judge UPI on cost and on knowing the money arrived; sound boxes that speak each payment exist for that reason. A zero fee leaves banks and apps unpaid for serving them, so merchants are the side every fee proposal targets.

payments confirmed at the counter · merchant churn to cash
BanksA share of the volume, and a way to pay for running it.
  • ₹1,500 croregovernment incentive, year to March 2025

Banks carry the load without the fee

Banks hold the accounts and run switches, yet earn no fee on a shop payment beyond this incentive. They are measured on success rate, and a slow bank shows up as declines on every app. Their tension is with the apps, which own the customer.

Apps are measured on monthly payers and on what they sell next: credit, insurance, ads on the receipt screen. NPCI is measured on uptime and on how widely volume is spread. Its cap pulls against the two leaders, and it has postponed the deadline twice.

The PM lens

“The success rate for UPI transactions drops by 5% over a weekend. How do you debug this?”

PhonePe PM interview · analytical round · reported to JobMentis, page updated 23 October 2026
What the round tests

Whether you can find the broken link in a chain you do not own.

  1. Split the failures by who said no.A wrong PIN or an empty account is a business decline; a timeout is a technical one. Only the second is a system problem.
  2. Walk the chain one link at a time.Your app, your partner bank, NPCI, the payers’ banks, the payees’ banks. A dip at one bank shows up as that bank’s line, not everyone’s.
  3. Check your own retries.A retry storm turns a wobble into an outage, as NPCI found in spring 2025. Count your status checks per payment before you blame the rail.
If this goes wrong

Blame the network and wait, while one bank’s weekend downtime costs a weekend of failed payments and merchants who switch apps.

The decision inside

Keep small payments free, charge only the big ones.

  1. StopA ₹20 chai paid by scanning a code, with no fee for the stall.About 800 million payments a day.
  2. NoticeFree payments still cost money to run, and the subsidy covers only payments up to ₹2,000.Someone pays for the rails, or nobody improves them.
  3. TradeoffStay free for everyone, or charge and risk pushing shops back to cash.Reach, or a way to pay for the rails.
  4. DecideA 0.4% fee on some merchant payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300; payments between people, payments up to ₹2,000 and small merchants stay free.What it cost: set for 15 October 2026, then deferred on 13 October to after Diwali, with no new date yet.
