How does a watch notice a heart problem before you do?

Five layers of a Series 11, from screen to heart sensor. The decision: how many irregular readings to wait for before the watch speaks.

Best for
  1. Catching a rhythm you cannot feelA clinic ECG sees one moment; this checks every two to four hours, and speaks only when five of six readings agree.
  2. Taking an ECG your cardiologist can readThirty seconds with a finger on the crown, saved to Health as a PDF, rather than waiting for a clinic slot.
  3. Watching over a parent who lives aloneFall and crash detection on their wrist, and a call for help if they do not answer within 10 seconds.

Less suited tothe athlete who wants a week of battery: Garmin wins there, and the health alerts are rarely opened.

Who uses it
  • The worried wellHealthy and data curious, wears it all day. Wants reassurance and a nudge, never a diagnosis.
  • The patient with a historyAlready on a cardiologist’s books. Wants a record a doctor will accept: the 30 second ECG as a PDF.
  • The athleteBuys it for workouts and battery. Health alerts are a bonus they rarely open.
  • The family buyerPuts one on a parent for fall detection, and for the alert that reaches them too.

Part 1 · The screen

The screen, and the case around it.

An always on OLED, 496 by 416 pixels on the 46 mm case, up to 2,000 nits in sunlight. The crown on the side doubles as an electrode.

Part 2 · The chip

The watch’s own computer.

The S10 chip runs checks on the wrist. On watches sold in the US since January 2024, blood oxygen is now worked out on the iPhone, after a patent ruling switched the feature off until August 2025.

Sources · Vodafone UK · The Register

PM angleWhere a calculation runs is a product decision. Moving one to the phone brought a feature back in the US.

Part 3 · The battery

A day of power, and every feature pays for it.

Up to 24 hours on Series 11, and 38 in Low Power Mode.

Source · Vodafone UK

PM angleA health feature that halves battery life gets worn less, and a watch on its charger measures nothing.

Part 4 · Motion sensors

Motion sensors that can tell a fall from a crash.

An accelerometer and a gyroscope. From Series 8 the accelerometer reads up to 256 g; after a crash, the watch calls for help if you do not answer within 10 seconds.

Source · TechCrunch

PM angleA false alarm here phones the emergency services. The threshold is a safety decision, not a tuning one.

Part 5 · The back

Light in, light back: the heart sensor.

Green lights flash hundreds of times a second and light sensors read how much comes back as blood pulses. Electrodes in the back and the crown record an ECG in 30 seconds.

Sources · Apple Support · Apple, via MacTech

PM angleBlood pressure alerts, cleared by the FDA on 11 September 2025, read 30 days of this signal. Apple expects to alert over a million people in the first year.

The watch stays quiet until the signal repeats: step 4 is the product decision.

Fig. 3 · The system behind a heart alert
  1. On your wrist

    Light reads your pulse while you sit still.

    • Optical heart sensorGreen light goes into the skin and sensors read what comes back. The method is called PPG.
    • Rhythm checkEvery two to four hours the gaps between beats are plotted, a chart called a tachogram, and judged on the watch.
    You own the sampling policy and the sensor roadmap

    Every new sensor competes for the same day of battery. A wearable PM ranks features by what they cost in hours as much as by what they detect.

    battery life after each release
  2. The rule

    It waits until most checks agree.

    • Five of sixFive of six readings within 48 hours must look irregular, the first included. After one does, checks come as often as every 15 minutes.
    The threshold is the decision, so date it

    Lower it and you catch more people, and send more healthy ones to a doctor. Any change goes through clinical and regulatory review, with a date, because the alert is a cleared medical feature.

    false alarms per 1,000 · time to a cleared change
  3. On your iPhone

    The alert, the history, and a recording a doctor can read.

    • NotificationThe watch speaks up; the Health app on the paired iPhone keeps the details.
    • Health storeHealth data is encrypted on the phone and closed to apps about ten minutes after it locks.
    • ECG appA 30 second recording from a finger on the crown, saved to Health and shared as a PDF.
    Where trust is won

    The alert, the history and a PDF a doctor can read. Privacy by default is the feature here: data locks ten minutes after the phone does, and that line is what lets people switch alerts on.

    share of wearers with alerts switched on
  4. In iCloud

    A copy only your devices can open.

    • End to end encryptionWith two factor authentication on, Health data in iCloud is encrypted so only your devices hold the keys.
    Sealed data means no silent learning

    End to end encryption keeps Apple out of the data, so the model improves through studies, not through use. That shapes the research plan and the pace of new alerts.

    study enrolment · months per cleared alert
In plain words

Like a smoke alarm that checks twice before it sounds. The watch takes a reading now and then, waits until five of six agree, and only then speaks up. It never names an illness; it points you to someone who can.

Design choices, and what they cost
  1. Judge on the wristReadings are classified on the watch, so no heartbeat waits on a server. The cost: it reads only when you are still enough, so a busy day can pass unchecked.
  2. Repeat before alertingFive of six trades speed for trust: in the Apple Heart Study, 84% of alerts agreed with atrial fibrillation. The cost: no alert in less than about an hour.

How it grew
The spike
Shipments rose 19% in 2020 and 27% in 2021 by Counterpoint’s count, and peaked near 43 million in 2022. Two years later they were 34 million, a fall of about a fifth.
What drove it
Series 6 with blood oxygen and the cheaper SE, both September 2020, widened the range at both ends. The 2024 slide came with the Masimo import ban and no new SE since 2022.
How they iterated
September 2022: Series 8 added temperature sensing and crash detection, and Ultra opened a higher tier. August 2025: blood oxygen returned in the US, computed on the iPhone. September 2025: FDA cleared hypertension alerts.

Apple Watch shipments a year, million units. Counterpoint for 2019 and 2020, via 9to5Mac; IDC for 2022 and 2024, via MacDailyNews. Apple publishes no unit figures.

In plain words
A smoke alarm tuned to stay quiet for toast.
Silence is the promise to the wearer

A smoke alarm earns trust by staying quiet through breakfast. The same holds on a wrist: people keep an alert switched on only while it rarely cries wolf. A run of false alarms and the watch is muted for good.

alerts dismissed without action · notifications muted
It optimises
Few false alarms: the blood pressure alert catches 41% of people with hypertension and stays silent for 92% of those without.
Specificity bought with missed cases

Catch more people with hypertension and more healthy wearers are sent to clinics. Stay quiet for the healthy and most of the sick hear nothing. A PM watches both rates and adds a third: how many act on the alert.

sensitivity · specificity
The dial
The threshold. Lower it to catch more people, and send more healthy ones to a doctor.
The line decides who pays for errors

Engineering can plot every point on the curve. Choosing one means deciding whose cost counts more: a worried healthy wearer, a busy clinic, or a missed diagnosis. That judgement sits with the PM, tested in a validation study before release.

clinic visits per alert · missed cases in validation
Same idea
Card fraud scoring: score every event, act only above a line.
Borrow the fraud team’s playbook

Card issuers learned that a declined honest purchase costs loyalty, so they score quietly and step in rarely. The lesson carries over: price each false alarm in lost trust, and review the line on a fixed schedule as the population shifts.

cost of a false alarm · drift in alert rate

“What’s your long term vision for the Apple Watch?”

What the round tests

Whether you can see years ahead and still name the constraint that decides the order.

  1. Name the job, not the gadget.The watch is becoming a health monitor that tells the time. The record shows the order: an ECG in 2018, crash detection in 2022, blood pressure alerts in 2025.
  2. Earn trust one clearance at a time.Each health claim passes the FDA before it reaches a wrist. The roadmap is a queue of clearances, and the bar is false alarms.
  3. Fit inside a day of battery.Every new sensor competes for the same 24 hours. A vision that ignores the charger is a slide, not a plan.
If this goes wrong

A feature can disappear overnight. Blood oxygen went dark on US watches for over a year, on a patent ruling, not a product one.

The decision inside

Speak only when the signal repeats.

  1. StopA watch that says your heart rhythm may be irregular.A sensor on millions of wrists, all day.
  2. NoticeA sensor that is right most of the time will still alarm healthy people every day.At this scale, a small error rate is a daily crowd.
  3. TradeoffCatch every case, or speak only when sure.Sensitive and noisy, or quiet and later.
  4. DecideStay quiet until five checks over at least 65 minutes agree; then the ECG app can take a 30 second reading.What it cost: some irregular rhythms are flagged later than the sensor alone could have.
