How does a watch know you are walking, not dancing?

A workout in five stages, from the wrist tap to the model that names it. The decision: which patterns are clear enough to name.

Best for
  1. Getting credit for an unplanned workoutA brisk walk to the station becomes exercise: a few minutes in it taps your wrist and counts the minutes already done.
  2. Tracking a swim with no phone nearbyIn the pool or on the rower the phone stays behind; the watch knows both patterns and logs the session alone.
  3. Keeping indoor distance honest without GPSTwenty minutes of outdoor walking teaches it your stride, so an indoor run reads right with no satellite in sight.

Less suited toyoga, dance and training load: the first two you start by hand, and Garmin owns the third.

Who uses it
  • The lapsed gym goerExercises in bursts and forgets to press start. Wants the watch to notice the effort anyway and close the rings without a ritual.
  • The runner with a planLives by pace and distance. Wants numbers that hold indoors and out, and a calorie count that survives comparison with a friend’s device.
  • The dancer and yoga studentDoes the workouts the watch cannot name and starts them by hand. Wants credit for effort that motion alone misses.
  • The Fitness+ subscriberPays monthly for classes on a screen. Wants the watch and the class to feel like one product, with the trainer reading their pulse.

Part 1 · The prompt

It noticed before you did.

After a few minutes of brisk movement the watch taps your wrist and offers to start a workout, then credits the minutes you had already done. It does the same when you forget to stop.

Part 2 · The accelerometer

The rhythm of an arm.

It measures movement along three axes, so walking and running show up as a steady swing. Outdoors, GPS teaches it your stride at each speed, which keeps distance right when GPS drops out.

Source · Apple Support

PM angleCalibration is invisible work: twenty minutes of walking outside makes every later indoor number better.

Part 3 · The gyroscope

Rotation, which dance is full of.

It measures how the wrist turns. For the Dance workout, Apple fuses it with the accelerometer to tell arm, leg and whole body movement apart, with heart rate as a check.

Source · AppleInsider

PM angleDance needed its own model because the wrist alone misses much of what the body does. A new workout type can mean new science, not a new icon.

Part 4 · The heart sensor

Effort that motion cannot show.

When the wrist barely moves, heart rate is what still shows effort; for Dance it confirms what the motion sensors see. Your height, weight, sex and age feed the calorie estimate too.

Sources · AppleInsider · Apple Support

PM angleTo a motion sensor, a hard plank and a nap look alike. The second sensor is what keeps the number honest.

Part 5 · The model

Only clear patterns are named.

Walks, runs, the elliptical, the rower and swims repeat in patterns the watch recognises, so it prompts for them; cycling joined in 2021. Yoga and dance do not, so you choose them yourself.

    The wait before asking sets the tone

    Ask too early and the watch nags people walking to the shops; ask late and the prompt feels slow. The delay is a tuned number, and the first thing to revisit if people switch detection off.

    Waiting a few minutes is only acceptable because nothing is lost. Without retroactive credit the delay would read as a bug. The two decisions are one, and should be reviewed together.

Step 3 is the gate: if the pattern is not one it knows, the watch stays quiet and you name the workout yourself.

Fig. 3 · The system behind a workout prompt
  1. On your wrist

    Sensors feel how your arm moves and how hard your heart works.

    • AccelerometerMovement along three axes: the steady swing of a walking arm.
    • GyroscopeRotation: the twists and turns that dance is full of.
    • Heart rate and GPSEffort that motion misses, and outdoors, real distance.
    Hardware is fixed, so start from it

    Nothing here can change in a software release. A wearable PM ranks ideas by what motion, heart rate and position can already tell apart, and files anything needing new silicon under a different, slower roadmap.

    features shipped on existing sensors
  2. The judgement

    The pattern is compared with workouts the watch knows.

    • Workout detectionPrompts only for clear patterns: walks, runs, swims, the rower, the elliptical, and cycling since 2021.
    • Sensor fusionFor Dance, both motion sensors are read together, with heart rate as a check, to tell arm, leg and whole body movement apart.
    • CalibrationOutdoor walks and runs teach it your stride at each speed, so indoor distance stays right.
    You own the threshold and the list

    Two dials live here: how sure the model must be before it prompts, and which workouts it is allowed to name. Both trade coverage for confidence, and the record shows the list growing slowly for a reason.

    prompts accepted per 100 shown · detection switched off
  3. The record

    Minutes, calories and the rings.

    • Calorie estimateYour height, weight, sex and age join the sensor data in each workout’s own model.
    • Health appThe finished workout is saved to Health, encrypted with the rest of your health data.
    The number people quote is an estimate

    Calories are what wearers compare across brands, yet they rest on four profile fields and a model nobody can audit. Watch for stale profiles: a weight entered years ago quietly skews every workout since.

    wearers with a profile updated this year
  4. Before launch

    The models learned from people in a lab.

    • Lab dataTo teach Dance, Apple measured dancers wearing masks that track breathing, with an iPhone on the hip.
    The lab is the real bottleneck

    Each new workout type that motion alone cannot name needs people measured in a lab. That sets the pace of the roadmap more than engineering does, so the lab calendar is a planning input.

    lab hours per new workout type
In plain words

Like a coach watching from the side of the pitch. A steady jog is easy to name; a salsa turn is not. So the watch speaks up only for the moves it recognises well, and leaves the rest to you.

Design choices, and what they cost
  1. Fuse sensors for hard casesDance needed both motion sensors read together, with the heart as a check. The cost: a new workout type can mean a new model and new lab data.
  2. Calibrate to the personGPS outdoors teaches stride length, so indoor distance needs no satellite. The cost: about 20 minutes outdoors before the numbers are at their best.

How it grew
The spike
Shipments grew 19% in 2020, to 33.9 million by Counterpoint’s count, and another 27% in 2021. They peaked near 43 million in 2022, then fell to 34 million by 2024.
What drove it
The lockdown year. Gyms shut, watchOS 7 learned to count dance and core training in September 2020, and Fitness+ launched that December with the watch as its pulse.

September 2022: watchOS 9 added running form metrics and heart rate zones. September 2023: watchOS 10 gave cyclists power data on the iPhone. June 2025: watchOS 26 announced Workout Buddy, a voice coach.

The PM lens

“How would you choose the health features Apple Watch should take on next?”

Apple PM interview · product design and strategy · reported to PracHub, 4 October 2026
What the round tests

Whether you rank ideas by what the sensors can truly tell apart, not by how good they sound.

  1. Start from the signal already on the wrist.Motion, heart rate and position are on every watch. A feature that needs a new sensor waits years for the hardware.
  2. Ship what can be told apart reliably first.Automatic detection began in 2018 with eight workouts and added cycling in 2021. Yoga and dance are still chosen by hand.
  3. Sequence by trust, not by reach.A wrong health prompt costs more than a missing one. Each feature should earn the right to the next.
If this goes wrong

Detect too eagerly and the watch nags people in the middle of errands. They switch detection off, and the good prompts go with the bad.

The decision inside

Name only what has a clear pattern.

  1. StopA watch that says you are walking, and never guesses yoga.It offers a workout before you ask.
  2. NoticeWalks, runs and swims repeat in steady rhythms. Yoga and dance do not.A wrist moves very differently in a downward dog and a salsa turn.
  3. TradeoffGuess every workout, or guess a few and ask about the rest.Coverage, or confidence.
  4. DecideDetect eight common workouts, add cycling in 2021, credit the minutes already done, and build a separate model for dance.What it cost: some workouts are never noticed unless you start them yourself.
